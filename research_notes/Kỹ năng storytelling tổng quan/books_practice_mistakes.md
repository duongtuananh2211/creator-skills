## Key question 1: Key books & authors (annotated), including Vietnamese translations

### Takeaway
The canon splits into four families: (1) screenwriting/structure (McKee, Campbell, Booker), (2) brain-science of story (Cron, Storr), (3) oral/personal storytelling (Dicks, The Moth), (4) business/persuasion/leadership story (Heath brothers, Simmons, Hall, Ganz). Several Vietnamese translations exist, but **two titles in the brief are wrong**: "Ý tưởng này là của chúng mình" is NOT Made to Stick, and I found no Vietnamese edition of McKee's *Story*.

**Annotated list — core idea of each**
- **Robert McKee — *Story: Substance, Structure, Style, and the Principles of Screenwriting* (1997).** Aimed at screenwriters but also novelists, playwrights, journalists — "anyone with a story to tell"; McKee is known for his "Story Seminar" — [Wikipedia: Robert McKee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_McKee); [SPAA catalog](https://discovery.spaa.ae/Record/3428?lng=vi). Core ideas **[BG]**: a story event creates meaningful change in a character's life expressed in terms of a *value* (life/death, love/hate) and achieved through *conflict*; the "gap" between expectation and result drives scenes; principles not formulas. ***Storynomics* (2018, with Thomas Gerace)** **[BG]**: applying story structure to marketing/business, arguing that interruptive advertising is declining and brands must tell stories.
- **Lisa Cron — *Wired for Story* (Ten Speed Press, 2012)**: uses neuroscience/cognitive science to explain how to "hook readers from the very first sentence"; sequel *Story Genius* — [PRH](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/215504/wired-for-story-by-lisa-cron/); [UNT catalog](https://discover.library.unt.edu/catalog/b6088708). *Story or Die* (VN: Kể chuyện hay là chết) covers how the brain processes info and builds story, understanding the audience, and building your own story — [Tiền Phong/Hoa Học Trò](https://hoahoctro.tienphong.vn/cuon-sach-nang-trinh-ke-chuyen-dinh-chop-cho-cac-nha-sang-tao-noi-dung-tre-post1668207.tpo). Core idea **[BG]**: a story is about how what happens affects someone pursuing a difficult goal and how they change internally — plot serves the protagonist's internal struggle.
- **Matthew Dicks — *Storyworthy* (2018)**: Dicks is a many-time Moth StorySLAM/GrandSLAM champion (counts vary by source date: 34, 35, 45, 50+); stories must show change over time, belong to the teller, and pass the "Dinner Test" — [SuperSummary](https://www.supersummary.com/storyworthy/summary/); counts discrepancy noted across [sources](https://annualmeeting.mdrt.org/speaker-sessions/matthew-dicks). Every great story centers on a **"five-second moment"** of transformation/realization; big events matter only insofar as they serve that tiny moment — [Glasp/Storyworthy](https://glasp.ai/discover/book/B07CV2PFYJ); [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/significant-moments/).
- **Chip & Dan Heath — *Made to Stick* (2007)**: six principles of sticky ideas = **SUCCESs**: Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Stories; ideas like "the Velcro theory of memory", "curiosity gap" — [Wikipedia: Made to Stick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Made_to_Stick); [Heath Brothers](https://heathbrothers.com/books/made-to-stick/); [PRH](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/77687/made-to-stick-by-chip-heath-and-dan-heath/). The "Curse of Knowledge" (experts can't imagine not knowing) is the book's central villain **[BG — well known but not re-fetched]**.
- **Annette Simmons — *The Story Factor* (2001; revised eds.)**: narrative persuades better than facts/bullet points; identifies **six stories you need to know how to tell**, running from "Who I am" to "I-know-what-you're-thinking"; includes "storylistening"; revised edition adds use in reorganizations, layoffs, diversity issues — [LibraryThing](https://www.librarything.com/work/1344640); [getAbstract](https://www.getabstract.com/summary/6082). Full six list **[BG, not confirmed]**: Who I Am, Why I Am Here, The Vision, Teaching, Values-in-Action, I Know What You Are Thinking. Simmons also warns against using story to manipulate **[BG]**.
- **Kindra Hall — *Stories That Stick* (2019)**: four business story types — **Value Story** (convince customers they need you), **Founder Story** (investors/customers), **Purpose Story** (align employees), **Customer Story** (users share authentic experiences); good stories need an identifiable character, authentic emotion, a significant moment, specific details — [Shortform overview](https://www.shortform.com/blog/kindra-hall-stories-that-stick/); [SHRM store](https://store.shrm.org/Stories-That-Stick). Structure "Normal → Explosion → New Normal" appears in a secondary summary — [Prezent blog](https://www.prezent.ai/blog/storytelling-in-business-kindra-hall) (secondary; plausible but not verified against book text).
- **Will Storr — *The Science of Storytelling* (2019 UK / 2020 Abrams US)**: blends psychology and neuroscience with literary examples (Harry Potter to King Lear) to explain how story works — [Poets & Writers](https://www.pw.org/node/1102639); [getAbstract](https://www.getabstract.com/en/summary/the-science-of-storytelling/39077). Core idea **[BG]**: the brain is a "hallucinated" model-maker; compelling characters have a "flawed theory of control"/sacred flaw, and the story is the dramatic question "who is this person?" as their flaw is tested by change.
- **Joseph Campbell — *The Hero with a Thousand Faces* (1949)**: comparative mythology; all myth tells one hero's journey — leaving the ordinary world, entering the supernatural, returning; influenced novelists and filmmakers — [Báo Quốc tế](https://baoquocte.vn/nguoi-hung-mang-ngan-guong-mat-dau-sach-kinh-dien-ve-van-hoa-van-hoc-o-my-148753.html). **[BG]** Popularized in Hollywood via Christopher Vogler's memo/*The Writer's Journey*; George Lucas cited it for Star Wars.
- **Christopher Booker — *The Seven Basic Plots* (2004)** **[BG, not fetched]**: argues all stories reduce to seven plots — Overcoming the Monster, Rags to Riches, The Quest, Voyage and Return, Comedy, Tragedy, Rebirth; took ~34 years to write; criticized for Jungian determinism.
- **Marshall Ganz — Public Narrative (Harvard Kennedy School)**: developed from his farmworker-movement organizing in the 1990s; **Story of Self** (who I am, my values, why I act), **Story of Us** (shared identity/values), **Story of Now** (present as a moment of challenge, hope, and choice → call to action); self/us stories center on past moments of challenge that required choice and had outcomes that taught a lesson — [Commons Library](https://commonslibrary.org/the-power-of-story-the-story-of-self-us-and-now/); [Beautiful Trouble](https://beautifultrouble.org/tool/story-of-self-us-and-now); [Ganz HKS exec ed PDF](https://scholar.harvard.edu/sites/scholar.harvard.edu/files/marshallganz/files/harvard_exec_ed_public_narrative_2020_final_.pdf); [Harvard DASH working paper](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037d-c817-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content); [Bloomberg Harvard City Leadership](https://www.cities.harvard.edu/?p=2858). Core structure **[BG]**: challenge → choice → outcome.
- **Stephen King — *On Writing: A Memoir of the Craft* (2000)**: part memoir, part craft guidance — [Netabooks](https://www.netabooks.vn/stephen-king-chuyen-nghe-viet-va-goc-khuat-cuoc-doi-cua-ong-hoang-sach-kinh-di); key practice ideas **[BG]**: read a lot and write a lot; "the road to hell is paved with adverbs"; second draft = first draft minus 10%; write with the door closed, rewrite with the door open; situation-first not plot-first. Relevance: written craft, less for oral/business storytelling.
- **The Moth — *How to Tell a Story* (2022, Moth staff)**: practical guide with chapters such as "Familiarization" and "Say It Out Loud" — [Blinkist](https://www.blinkist.com/books/how-to-tell-a-story-en); [Goodreads notes](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/59000869-how-to-tell-a-story/3315851-roozbeh-daneshvar). Not in the brief but arguably the most practical oral-storytelling manual.

### Inferences
- For a Vietnamese audience, the books actually readable in Vietnamese are: Made to Stick (Tạo ra thông điệp kết dính – likely out of print), Campbell (Người hùng mang ngàn gương mặt), King (Chuyện nghề viết), Cron's Story or Die (Kể chuyện hay là chết), Kindra Hall (Content Storytelling…). McKee, Storyworthy, Wired for Story, Storr, Booker, Simmons appear English-only as far as I could check.
- Suggested reading path for a creator: Storyworthy (find & tell personal stories) → Made to Stick (make ideas stick) → Wired for Story / Story or Die (why it works) → Storr (character) → McKee (structure depth) → Hall/Simmons/Ganz (business, leadership, mission).

## Key question 2: How to practice / develop storytelling ability

### Takeaway
The most-cited concrete regimen is Dicks' **Homework for Life** (daily 5-minute logging of the most storyworthy moment) feeding a **story bank**, combined with **telling stories out loud** repeatedly (The Moth: told from memory, ~5 minutes, no notes), then refining structure (5-second moment, but/therefore) and getting feedback in live settings (open mics, StorySLAMs).

### Cited Findings
- **Homework for Life (Dicks)**: spend ~5 minutes at the end of each day picking the single most storyworthy moment and recording it in a sentence or two (Dicks uses a spreadsheet with the date and a few words) — [Pfeiffer Law blog](https://pfeifferlaw.com/entertainment-law-blog/matthew-dicks-homework-for-life); [SoBrief/Storyworthy](https://sobrief-staging.onrender.com/books/storyworthy); [Mary Ann Moore](https://www.maryannmoore.ca/?p=6556)
- **Dinner Test (Dicks)**: rehearse/perform the story the way you'd tell it to a friend over dinner — no theatrical gestures or overwrought language — [SuperSummary](https://www.supersummary.com/storyworthy/summary/); [Learning Leader podcast ep. 430](https://poderato.com/shows/the-learning-leader-show-with-ryan-hawk/430-matthew-dicks-change-your-life-through-the-power-of-storytelling)
- **Find the 5-second moment first**, then build the story backwards around it — [Shortform: point of a story](https://www.shortform.com/blog/point-of-a-story/); [Glasp](https://glasp.ai/discover/book/B07CV2PFYJ)
- **Oral practice, Moth-style**: stories are told out loud, first person, from memory — not read or recited; no notes or props; ~5 minutes (with a 1-minute buffer); practice telling it aloud — [Seattle's Child (Moth challenge)](https://www.seattleschild.com/moth-storytelling-challenge-at-home/); [getAbstract journal on Moth](https://journal.getabstract.com/?p=93007). The Moth's book has chapters "Familiarization" and "Say It Out Loud" — [Blinkist](https://www.blinkist.com/books/how-to-tell-a-story-en)
- **Revision pass with but/therefore**: after drafting, go back through the beats and replace every "and then" with "but" or "therefore" — attributed to Matt Stone/Trey Parker — [The Hustle](https://thehustle.co/storytelling-secrets-netted-matt-stone-trey-parker-600-million); [Perell](https://perell.com/note/but-therefore-rule/)
- **Public narrative worksheet practice (Ganz)**: write a Story of Self around a specific moment of challenge → choice → outcome, then connect to Us and Now; used in HKS exec ed and with mayors — [Ganz exec ed PDF](https://scholar.harvard.edu/sites/scholar.harvard.edu/files/marshallganz/files/harvard_exec_ed_public_narrative_2020_final_.pdf); [UNY Public Narrative worksheet 2026](https://www.unyumc.org/images/uploads/REVISED_UNY_laity_session_Public_Narrative_worksheet_2026.pdf); [Bloomberg Harvard](https://www.cities.harvard.edu/?p=2858)
- **Kindra Hall's checklist as a drafting tool**: does the story have an identifiable character, authentic emotion, a significant moment, specific details? — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/kindra-hall-stories-that-stick/)

**Background methods [BG — standard practice, not re-sourced this session]**
- *Story bank*: keep a categorized list (failures, firsts, turning points, customer moments) built from Homework for Life + childhood "crossroads" exercise from Storyworthy (map pivotal choice points).
- *Record and review*: record voice/video of a telling, transcribe, cut filler, time it; compare versions.
- *Retelling exercise*: tell the same story to 3 different listeners in a week; note where attention dips; tell a famous story/film in 60 s, 3 min, 5 min.
- *Improv*: "Yes, and" (accept and build) trains listening, spontaneity, and building on offers; improv "story spine" (Kenn Adams): "Once upon a time… Every day… But one day… Because of that… Because of that… Until finally… And ever since then…" — popularized by Pixar's Emma Coats.
- *Show-don't-tell drills*: rewrite an abstract sentence ("I was nervous") as observable action/sensory detail (sweaty palms, re-reading the same line).
- *Sensory/specificity drill*: 5 senses list per scene; replace generic nouns with named specifics ("a car" → "my dad's rusting 1998 Corolla").
- *Dialogue*: replace summary ("she told me no") with a short exchange of actual lines.
- *5 Ws opener*: establish who/where/when quickly and physically (Dicks: open with forward motion, a location).
- *Deliberate practice (Ericsson)*: specific sub-skill targets, immediate feedback, practice at edge of ability, repetition — applies to storytelling via one-variable-at-a-time rehearsals (e.g., only work on the opening line this week).
- *Live feedback loops*: Moth StorySLAM open mics (themes announced in advance, names drawn from a hat, judged by audience teams), Toastmasters, local storytelling nights.

### Inferences
- A plausible 30-day plan (synthesized, not from a single cited coach):
  - Days 1–30 (daily, 5 min): Homework for Life entry.
  - Week 1: Build story bank (≥20 moments); pick 3; identify each one's 5-second moment and "who was I before / after".
  - Week 2: Draft one story; but/therefore beat pass; cut backstory; first line = in the action, last line = the change. Tell it aloud daily, recorded; Dinner Test.
  - Week 3: Tell it to 3+ live listeners; collect feedback ("where did you lean in / drift?"); add sensory detail, dialogue, stakes; trim to 5 min.
  - Week 4: Perform at an open mic/team meeting/video; draft story #2 (a Ganz Self–Us–Now or Hall Value/Founder story for business use).
- I found no authoritative, sourced "30-day storytelling plan used by coaches"; the above is a synthesis.

- No primary source fetched for improv "Yes, and"/Story Spine, Ericsson deliberate practice applied to storytelling, or recording-and-review studies. The Moth's own tips page returned HTTP 403.
- No empirical evidence found on effectiveness of Homework for Life beyond Dicks' own claims and testimonials.

## Key question 3: Core elements / craft principles

### Takeaway
Across sources the convergent model is: **a specific character who wants something → obstacle/conflict raises stakes → a turning point → change (internal or external)**, told with concrete specifics and causal linkage (but/therefore), centered on one moment of transformation.

### Cited Findings
- **Change/transformation is mandatory**: a story must reflect change over time (Dicks) — [SuperSummary](https://www.supersummary.com/storyworthy/summary/); the five-second moment of transformation/realization/revelation is the core — [Glasp](https://glasp.ai/discover/book/B07CV2PFYJ)
- **Stakes/tension**: without stakes, Moth stories "lack tension and will fall flat"; stakes can be internal tensions or external pressures — [getAbstract journal](https://journal.getabstract.com/?p=93007)
- **Causality — but/therefore rule**: "but" introduces conflict, "therefore" leads to consequence/new action; "and then" makes stories dull and list-like — [Perell](https://perell.com/note/but-therefore-rule/); [Goodreads blog on Parker's documentary comment](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/8414248-the-key-to-story-structure-in-two-words-therefore-but?tab=author); [Wikipedia: Transition (fiction)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_(fiction)). **Attribution caveat**: widely said to come from a Parker/Stone appearance at an NYU class (the "Stone & Parker NYU lecture" is reported by [Matt Rickard](https://blog.matt-rickard.com/p/but-therefore) as paraphrase); a primary recording was not located — treat the NYU detail as unverified, though the rule itself is clearly attributed to them in multiple reports including on-camera remarks in a short documentary.
- **Concreteness, unexpectedness, emotion, credibility** (SUCCESs) — [Wikipedia: Made to Stick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Made_to_Stick)
- **Character + emotion + moment + specifics** (Hall) — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/kindra-hall-stories-that-stick/)
- **Challenge → choice → outcome; values made visible** (Ganz) — [Bloomberg Harvard](https://www.cities.harvard.edu/?p=2858); [Commons Library](https://commonslibrary.org/the-power-of-story-the-story-of-self-us-and-now/)
- **The story must belong to the teller** (personal, not borrowed) — [SuperSummary](https://www.supersummary.com/storyworthy/summary/)
- **Brain-hooking**: Cron frames story craft in terms of what the brain is wired to attend to — [PRH](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/215504/wired-for-story-by-lisa-cron/)

**[BG]** Additional principles commonly taught:
- Desire: protagonist wants something specific (Cron, McKee).
- Vulnerability: Dicks/Moth stress the teller should not be the hero who always wins; stories about your own failures/smallness connect more.
- Tension and release; "elephant" (Dicks: signal early what the story is about / what's at stake); "breadcrumbs" and "hourglass" (slow down right before the big moment) in Storyworthy.
- Present tense for immediacy (Dicks recommends).
- Pixar's 22 rules (Emma Coats, 2011) — e.g., "You admire a character for trying more than for their successes."

### Inferences
- A compact checklist for the final report: **Ai? Muốn gì? Cái gì cản? Mất gì nếu thất bại? Khoảnh khắc thay đổi là gì? Trước/sau khác nhau ra sao? Chi tiết cụ thể nào chứng minh?**

### Gaps
- Dicks' terms "elephant", "hourglass", "breadcrumbs", "crystal ball" not confirmed via fetched source in this session.

## Key question 4: Common mistakes and fixes (including ethics)

### Takeaway
The recurring failures are: no stakes/conflict, no change, an "and then" list of events, too much context, generic language, performing instead of talking, reading/reciting, running long, and — in business — making the brand/teller the hero or fabricating/manipulating.

### Cited Findings & fixes
| Mistake | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|
| No stakes → no tension, falls flat | Name what could be lost/gained; internal or external stakes | [getAbstract journal (Moth)](https://journal.getabstract.com/?p=93007) |
| No change / just an anecdote | Find the 5-second moment; show who you were before vs after | [Glasp](https://glasp.ai/discover/book/B07CV2PFYJ); [SuperSummary](https://www.supersummary.com/storyworthy/summary/) |
| Episodic "and then… and then" | Replace with "but"/"therefore" — every beat caused by the previous | [The Hustle](https://thehustle.co/storytelling-secrets-netted-matt-stone-trey-parker-600-million); [Perell](https://perell.com/note/but-therefore-rule/) |
| Theatrical, over-written delivery | Dinner Test: tell it like to a friend over dinner | [SuperSummary](https://www.supersummary.com/storyworthy/summary/) |
| Reading notes / memorizing word-for-word | Tell from memory, not read or recited; no notes/props; practice aloud | [Seattle's Child](https://www.seattleschild.com/moth-storytelling-challenge-at-home/); [getAbstract journal](https://journal.getabstract.com/?p=93007) |
| Too long | Hard time box (~5 min Moth format) and cut | [Seattle's Child](https://www.seattleschild.com/moth-storytelling-challenge-at-home/) |
| Abstract/generic language | Be Concrete (SUCCESs); specific details | [Made to Stick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Made_to_Stick); [Shortform/Hall](https://www.shortform.com/blog/kindra-hall-stories-that-stick/) |
| Telling someone else's story / borrowed story | Story must belong to the teller | [SuperSummary](https://www.supersummary.com/storyworthy/summary/) |
| Facts/bullets instead of narrative when persuading | Use story; Simmons argues narrative persuades better than facts | [LibraryThing](https://www.librarything.com/work/1344640) |
| Personal story disconnected from the audience's cause | Ganz: link Self → Us → Now with a concrete call to action | [Commons Library](https://commonslibrary.org/the-power-of-story-the-story-of-self-us-and-now/) |

**[BG — not re-sourced]**
- *Too much backstory/context up front*: start in motion (Dicks: start as close to the end as possible / with forward movement); add context only when needed.
- *No clear point*: write the one-sentence "what changed" before drafting (Dicks: know your 5-second moment and build to it; Moth: know your ending).
- *Teller as hero in marketing*: Donald Miller's *Building a StoryBrand* (2017) — the customer is the hero, the brand is the guide (Yoda, not Luke). Kindra Hall's Customer Story makes the same move.
- *Weak ending / summarizing moral*: end on the change; don't explain the moral at length; last line should land on the 5-second moment.
- *Telling instead of showing*: replace adjectives about feelings with actions, dialogue, sensory detail.
- *Making yourself look good*: vulnerability; Dicks' advice that stories where you are the smartest/bravest rarely work.
- *Ethics — fabrication*: The Moth requires true stories; high-profile cases of fabricated "true" stories (e.g., Mike Daisey's Apple/Foxconn monologue retracted by *This American Life* in 2012) show reputational damage. Distinguish compression/composites (disclose) from invention.
- *Ethics — manipulation ("storyselling")*: Simmons distinguishes influence from manipulation; Storr/Cron note stories bypass critical reasoning — power implies responsibility. Use true, representative customer stories; don't manufacture emotional triggers detached from the product's real value.

### Inferences
- For creators/marketers, the two most costly mistakes are probably (1) no stakes/no change (the content is "information with a character"), and (2) brand-as-hero — fix with Hall's Customer/Value stories and Ganz's Self→Us→Now bridge.
