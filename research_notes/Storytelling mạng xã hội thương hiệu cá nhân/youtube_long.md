Research date: 2026-10-29. Tags: [OFFICIAL] = YouTube/Google first-party; [STUDY] = academic/peer-reviewed or primary data org; [PRACTITIONER] = creator/strategist/agency (may be self-reported or vendor); [BG] = background knowledge, not verified in this session. ## 1. What does YouTube officially say? (retention, intros, title/thumbnail, Test & Compare, satisfaction)

### Takeaway
YouTube's official line is consistent: packaging has to match the content, the first 30 seconds are measured as their own metric ("Intro" in the key-moments report), and the system optimizes for watch time that viewers *value* (measured partly with 1–5 star surveys), not raw clicks. Test & Compare picks winners by **watch time**, not CTR, so a "clickbait" thumbnail that earns clicks but loses viewers can lose the test.

### Cited Findings

**Audience retention / "Key moments for audience retention" report**
- [OFFICIAL] "The key moments for audience retention report explains how well different moments of your video held viewers' attention." — [YouTube Help: Measure key moments for audience retention](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)
- [OFFICIAL] "Intro tells you what percentage of your audience still watched your video after the first 30 seconds." The page ties a high intro % to content matching the title/thumbnail. — [YouTube Help 9314415](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)
- [OFFICIAL] Advice for weak intros: revise thumbnail and title "so they better match the video's content" and "Modify the first 30 seconds of your video and experiment with different styles". — [YouTube Help 9314415](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)
- [OFFICIAL] "Top moments are moments in your video where almost no one dropped off while watching." "Spikes are moments in your video that were rewatched or shared" (a spike can also mean confusion → rewatch). "Dips highlight moments in your video that were either skipped" or where viewers left. — [YouTube Help 9314415](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)
- [OFFICIAL] "When the line on the chart is flat, it means viewers are watching that part of your video from start to finish." "Gradual declines mean that viewers are losing interest over time." "Videos on YouTube generally taper off during the playback period." — [YouTube Help 9314415](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)
- [OFFICIAL] If top moments appear late, move compelling content earlier because "audience sizes typically decrease over the length of the video"; make new content that expands on top moments. — [YouTube Help 9314415](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)
- [OFFICIAL] Key moments only appear for videos ≥60 seconds with ≥100 views; "typical retention" compares against your 10 latest videos of similar length. — [YouTube Help 9314415](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)

**Intro guidance (old, 2011)**
- [OFFICIAL, 2011 — old] Creator Playbook "first 15 seconds" (Ryan Nugent, Aug 27, 2011): "Start off with something that will immediately grab attention, whether it's what you say or a stunning visual"; "Make it clear what your video is about early on, so viewers aren't confused about what they're watching"; "Tease the rest of the video so the audience is intrigued to see where you take them."; "A flashy intro may look cool, but it's not the star of the video - let them see you, or your great content, first"; "Make your branding compelling by making it entertaining or unique to each video". No specific branding duration is given in the original. — [blog.youtube: YouTube Creator Playbook tips: the first 15 seconds](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-creator-playbook-tips-first-15)
- [PRACTITIONER] Secondary coverage claims YouTube found ~5 seconds is good branding length; this number is NOT in the blog.youtube original I fetched — treat as unverified. — [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/youtube-creator-playbook-on-the-first-15-seconds/)

**Recommendation system: watch time vs satisfaction (2021 — still the main first-party explainer)**
- [OFFICIAL, 2021] Cristos Goodrow (VP Engineering), Sep 15, 2021: "Clicking on a video provides a strong indication that you will also find it satisfying." "But we learned back in 2011 that clicking on a video doesn't mean you actually watched it." — [blog.youtube: On YouTube's recommendation system](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/)
- [OFFICIAL, 2021] "When we first incorporated watchtime into recommendations, we saw an immediate 20% drop in views." — same source
- [OFFICIAL, 2021] "we measure what we call 'valued watchtime'—the time spent watching a video that you consider valuable." "We measure valued watchtime through user surveys that ask you to rate the video you watched from one to five stars." "Only videos that you rate highly with four or five stars are counted as valued watchtime." — same source
- [OFFICIAL, 2021] "We don't want viewers regretting the videos they spend time watching." "On average, people are more likely to be satisfied by videos that they share or like." Dislikes are a negative signal. The system learns "from over 80 billion pieces of information we call signals" and "doesn't follow a set formula". — same source
- [OFFICIAL, 2021] The post mentions demoting "sensationalistic tabloid content" on homepages; it does not use the word "clickbait". — same source
- [PRACTITIONER, secondhand] Todd Beaupré (YouTube Growth & Discovery) to Rene Ritchie: "We're trying to understand not just about the viewer's behavior and what they do, but how they feel about the time they're spending." Coverage lists satisfaction inputs: post-view surveys, return visits, shares, "Don't recommend channel". I could not retrieve a primary Creator Insider transcript. — reported via [IBTimes SG](https://www.ibtimes.sg/A000Nka), [Lia Haberman](https://liahaberman.substack.com/p/icymi-how-youtubes-2025-algorithm)
- [PRACTITIONER — vendor, unverified] Claims that "satisfaction replaced watch time" as the main signal in 2025/2026 come from SEO/tool vendors and are not backed by a YouTube announcement I could find. — e.g. [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-viewer-satisfaction-algorithm-2026/), [Virvid](https://virvid.ai/blog/viewer-satisfaction-signals-youtube-2026)

**Test & Compare (A/B for thumbnails and titles)**
- [OFFICIAL] "Creators can test and compare up to 3 different titles and thumbnails." Test titles only, thumbnails only, or both. — [YouTube Help 13861714](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714)
- [OFFICIAL] Winner = option "with the highest watch time will be shown to all viewers." The page explains watch time is used rather than CTR because good packaging should help viewers find videos they'll actually watch. — same source
- [OFFICIAL] Result labels (current page): Winner (statistically significant), Performed Same, Inconclusive (first-uploaded becomes default). "Your test should be completed within two weeks." Changing title/thumbnail mid-test stops it. Desktop Studio only; needs advanced features; not for Shorts, scheduled lives, premieres (until converted), made-for-kids, mature, private. Tips: test older videos first; make options meaningfully different; thumbnails ≥1280×720 (smaller are downscaled to 854×480); a small control group may see only the default. — same source
- Conflict: [PRACTITIONER] third-party write-ups from the thumbnail-only era describe "watch time share" and labels "Winner / Preferred / None". The current official page uses "Winner / Performed Same / Inconclusive". Use the official labels. — [thumbnailtest.com (vendor)](https://thumbnailtest.com/news/youtube-releases-title-test-compare/), [Social Media Today on title testing](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-adds-title-testing-youtube-studio/753015/)

### Inferences
- For a scoring skill, the "Intro" metric (= % still watching at 0:30) is the one official retention number a creator can target directly. The official remedy pairs *packaging match* with *first-30s rewrite*: score both together.
- Because Test & Compare optimizes watch time, a skill that generates 3 title/thumbnail variants should make them meaningfully different (angle, not wording) and each must be "true to the video". Clickbait that wins CTR but loses viewers will lose.
- "Valued watch time" (4–5 star) means payoff and satisfaction at the end matter, not just holding attention. A draft-scoring rubric should include "did the video deliver what the package promised?"

### Gaps
- No primary Creator Insider video/transcript on satisfaction surveys was retrieved; Beaupré quotes are secondhand.
- No current (2024–2026) official YouTube article giving an intro length in seconds. The 2011 Playbook is the latest official intro-specific guidance I found.
- I did not retrieve YouTube's official title/thumbnail best-practice page (e.g., Help "Add video thumbnails" / misleading metadata policy). The spam/deceptive-practices policy on misleading thumbnails [BG] exists but wasn't fetched.

## 2. Intro structure for the first 30–60 seconds

### Takeaway
Evidence (official + MrBeast doc) supports: open on the most compelling thing, confirm immediately that the video is what the title/thumbnail promised, tease where it's going, delay branding, and switch from "telling" to "showing" fast (by ~1 min). Specific timing formulas ("hook in 5 seconds", "45-second intro formula") are practitioner rules of thumb, not measured standards.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] First 30 seconds are tracked as "Intro"; the fix for low intro retention is to match title/thumbnail and rework the first 30s. — [YouTube Help 9314415](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)
- [OFFICIAL, 2011] Grab attention immediately (words or visual), make the topic clear early, tease the rest, don't lead with a flashy branded intro. — [blog.youtube first 15 seconds](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-creator-playbook-tips-first-15)
- [PRACTITIONER, leaked internal doc, ~2022] MrBeast "How to Succeed in MrBeast Production": "The first minute of each video is the most important minute of each video." "On this particular video we lost 21 million viewers in the first minute of the video." The title/thumbnail "set the expectations for the viewer"; viewers "click off because the video did not meet the expectations of the title/thumbnail." "THIS IS WHY YOU MUST KNOW THE TITLE AND THUMBNAILS OF THE VIDEOS YOU ARE MAKING!" — [Alexander Jarvis transcript of memo](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Same doc on minutes 1–3: "This is where you have to transition from hype to execution (generally)." "Stop telling people what they will be watching and start showing them." — [Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- Authenticity of the doc: Rosanna Pansino posted the 36-page doc (Aug 2024); Passionfruit spoke with two former MrBeast producers who said it was legitimate; MrBeast's spokesperson declined comment; believed written ~2022. — [Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/mrbeast-leaked-document-81151/), [NetInfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/mrbeast-productions-leaked-internal-document-rosanna-pansino/)
- [PRACTITIONER, single secondary source] Paddy Galloway reportedly focuses heavily on intros and uses unanswered questions to keep viewers; a podcast chapter references a "45-second intro formula" whose content I could not verify. — [Scaleviews newsletter](https://scaleviews.beehiiv.com/p/how-paddy-galloway-forever-broke-the-youtube-algorithm), [Startup Ideas Podcast listing](https://audioscrape.com/podcast/the-startup-ideas-podcast/episode/this-youtube-expert-made-creators-100m-not-clickbait)
- [PRACTITIONER, vendor] Epidemic Sound recommends a strong start in the first 5 seconds; Teleprompter.com suggests 3–8 second hook+promise intros. Rules of thumb, not studies. — [Epidemic Sound](https://www.epidemicsound.com/blog/how-to-hook-your-viewers/), [Teleprompter](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/how-long-should-a-youtube-intro-be)
- [BG] Common creator-educator intro anatomy: (1) confirm the click (restate the thumbnail/title promise in the first sentence or show it), (2) stakes/why it matters to the viewer, (3) credibility or the personal angle, (4) open loop ("by the end you'll see…"/a question answered later), (5) start content—no "hey guys, welcome back, don't forget to subscribe" before value.

### Inferences
- A practical intro template for the skill (synthesis, not a proven formula): **0–5s** visual/verbal proof of the promise → **5–20s** stakes + who this is for → **20–40s** open loop(s) + roadmap → **≤45–60s** start delivering. Branding/subscribe asks go later.
- "Folklore vs evidence": the *direction* (fast, matched, teased) is supported by official + MrBeast; the *exact seconds* are folklore. The skill should present seconds as targets, not rules.
- Personal-brand creators: the "credibility/personal stake" beat (why *I* am telling this) is the brand lever, but must stay short.

### Gaps
- No public, controlled study comparing intro lengths/styles on YouTube retention.
- Galloway's "45-second intro formula" not verified from a primary source.

## 3. Body structures: escalation, chapters, re-hooks, but/therefore, MrBeast, Galloway, Veritasium, Harris/Neistat, documentary narration

### Takeaway
The best-documented body rules come from the MrBeast doc: front-load the best simple content, re-engage around minute 3 and minute 6, escalate stakes, keep the end strong and abrupt, and don't signal the end. Add the South Park "but/therefore" causality test to keep beats connected. Personal-storytelling styles (Johnny Harris, Casey Neistat) are well known but I found no primary breakdowns in this session.

### Cited Findings
**MrBeast production doc (leaked, ~2022) — [PRACTITIONER]** — all from [Alexander Jarvis transcript](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- Metrics: CTR, AVD, AVP. "For your videos to do well you must get their AVD and AVP as high as possible."
- Minutes 1–3: "We'd tried to cover multiple days in the first 3 minutes of the video" (fast early progression); re-engagement segment framed as "'only MrBeast can do this'".
- Minutes 3–6: "This is where you plan out all the most exciting and interesting content that is also very simple." "If we can get them to watch the first half of the video there's a very high chance they'll watch to the end." "Usually at the 6 minute mark we will include another re-engagement."
- Back half: "Typically the not as good content would be in the back half of the video." "Don't ever signal the end of the video unless it's to build hype for the prize or payoff at the end of the video." "The video endings must always be abrupt to protect retention."
- Stakes: "As you get deeper in the video the stakes get higher." "Strong payoffs at the end of videos boost retention."
- Clarity: "for 50 million people to understand something it must be simple." Also "No dull moments in videos," "You can't fake intensity in videos."
- Retention evidence: two similar-length videos, 120M vs 45M views; on the bigger one "People on average watched this video a minute and 38 seconds longer". Brand deal: "Notice the crater where the brand deal is"; integrated style gives "a dip [that] is way less dramatic."
- Secondary summaries describe re-engagement "at 3 and 6 minutes." — [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240917-mrbeast-youtube-production), [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/leaked-document-allegedly-reveals-mrbeasts-secrets-to-youtube-success-the-key-takeaways/)

**But/therefore causality**
- [PRACTITIONER] Trey Parker & Matt Stone, NYU Tisch guest lecture (2011, aired on mtvU "Stand In"): if "and then" fits between beats, "that's not a movie"; beats should be linked by "but" and "therefore". Note: the speakola transcript uses "but"/"because"; summaries use "but"/"therefore". — [The Hustle](https://thehustle.co/storytelling-secrets-netted-matt-stone-trey-parker-600-million), [Speakola NYU tag](https://speakola.com/arts/tag/NYU), [Perell](https://perell.com/?p=2244)

**Official support for re-hooks**
- [OFFICIAL] Retention typically tapers; dips show skipped/abandoned segments; move compelling content earlier. — [YouTube Help 9314415](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)

**Paddy Galloway**
- [PRACTITIONER] Galloway's public material focuses on ideation and packaging more than body structure. The Colin & Samir summary contains "no specific claims" on intros, retention benchmarks, or ideal length. — [Colin & Samir: The New Rules of YouTube From Paddy Galloway](https://www.colinandsamir.com/resources/the-new-rules-of-youtube-from-paddy-galloway)
- [PRACTITIONER] "80% rule": ~80% of videos aimed at the existing audience, ~20% experiments; set expectations before upload. Core/Casual/New (CCN) audience framing. — [Colin & Samir](https://www.colinandsamir.com/resources/the-new-rules-of-youtube-from-paddy-galloway), [Scaleviews](https://scaleviews.beehiiv.com/p/how-paddy-galloway-forever-broke-the-youtube-algorithm)

**Veritasium / Derek Muller**
- [PRACTITIONER, secondary] "Clickbait is unreasonably effective" (Veritasium, Aug 17, 2021, ~19:25; working title "We Need To Talk About Clickbait"). Summaries say Muller distinguishes enticing-but-accurate "legit bait" from misleading "click traps", and argues accurate bait helps science reach wider audiences; also discusses "anti-clickbait" where the title's question is answered in the thumbnail. — [TheTVDB listing](https://thetvdb.com/series/veritasium/episodes/8617959), [fbpe mirror/summary](https://old.fbpe.org/clickbait-is-unreasonably-effective.html), [Digital Information World](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2021/09/why-is-clickbait-so-common-in-online.html)
- [BG] Veritasium body pattern: open with a counterintuitive question/demo, run the story through history/people (who discovered it, what went wrong), build to the answer with a twist; the question is answered late, not in the intro.

**Johnny Harris / Casey Neistat / documentary narration**
- [BG] Johnny Harris: first-person "I wanted to understand X" framing, investigation-as-plot (questions → reporting → map/visual reveals), heavy B-roll, maps, archival, on-camera reflection.
- [BG] Casey Neistat: "every video should tell a story" — beginning/middle/end even in daily vlogs; conflict or a goal; fast cutting, timelapses, voice-over.
- [PRACTITIONER] Galloway cites Johnny Harris's "$25K vs $25M" comparison format as "familiar but unexpected" packaging. — [Colin & Samir](https://www.colinandsamir.com/resources/the-new-rules-of-youtube-from-paddy-galloway)
- [BG] Documentary narration conventions: cold open on the most dramatic moment, then "to understand how we got here, we have to go back…"; chapter cards; each chapter ends on an unresolved question that the next answers.

**Chapters**
- [BG] YouTube supports chapters via timestamps in the description (first timestamp 00:00, at least 3 chapters, each ≥10s). Not re-verified this session.

### Inferences
- Body skeleton for the outline skill (synthesis): Intro (0–1 min) → Act 1: setup + first win (1–3) → **re-hook ~3 min** → Act 2: escalating stakes, best "simple" content (3–6) → **re-hook ~6 min** → for 8–30 min videos, a re-hook every ~2–4 min / at each chapter break → climax/payoff → abrupt end (no "that's it for today" before the payoff).
- Each beat transition passes the but/therefore test; any "and then" transition flags a likely dip.
- A re-hook = new open loop, stakes raise, pattern break (visual/format change), or a teased reveal ("the third one surprised me"). Use YouTube's dip report to place them after publishing.
- Sponsor segments cause measurable dips (MrBeast "crater"); integrate them into the story or place after a strong open loop.
- MrBeast rules come from an entertainment-challenge format; for personal-brand educational/story videos, "abrupt endings" and "extreme" packaging should be softened. Flag the context in the skill.

### Gaps
- No primary-source analysis of Johnny Harris or Casey Neistat storytelling retrieved; claims are [BG].
- No primary Derek Muller transcript retrieved; quotes are via secondary summaries.
- No empirical data on optimal re-hook interval for 8–30 min non-MrBeast videos.

## 4. Packaging: title formulas, curiosity gap, thumbnail text, alignment, clickbait vs satisfaction

### Takeaway
Packaging (title + thumbnail) is the biggest lever per practitioners, and the official system rewards packaging that leads to watch time and satisfaction. The curiosity gap works when the viewer knows enough to feel the gap and believes the video will close it. Title, thumbnail, and first 30 seconds should be one promise; mismatch shows up as low "Intro" retention.

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTITIONER] Galloway: "The difference between a million views and 28 million views is how you package it." Top creators reportedly spend ~30% of time on ideation+packaging vs ~5% for small creators (who spend ~95% filming/editing). — [Colin & Samir](https://www.colinandsamir.com/resources/the-new-rules-of-youtube-from-paddy-galloway)
- [PRACTITIONER] Curiosity-gap case: Ian Lauer's "I photographed the Milky Way" → "Photographing the Milky Way in 10 minutes, 1 hour, and 24 hours"; views went from ~2–3K/video to 1M+. Formats that work: superlatives, matchups, three-tier comparisons ("$1 vs $100", "10 minutes vs 24 hours"), specific/unique lists. "Familiar but unexpected": borrow a proven format from another niche. — [Colin & Samir](https://www.colinandsamir.com/resources/the-new-rules-of-youtube-from-paddy-galloway)
- [PRACTITIONER, self-reported] Re-packaging old videos: Tim Gabe's thumbnail change gave ~30–40% improvement and ~40x more views/day. — same source
- [PRACTITIONER] MrBeast doc: "I Spent" → "I Survived" raised CTR; "In general the more extreme the better" (in the context of MrBeast's challenge format); the package sets expectations, unmet expectations cause click-offs. — [Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- [OFFICIAL] Weak intro retention → "revise your thumbnail and title so they better match the video's content". — [YouTube Help 9314415](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)
- [OFFICIAL] Test & Compare chooses by watch time, not CTR. — [YouTube Help 13861714](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714)
- [OFFICIAL, 2021] Clicks don't equal satisfaction; valued watch time is survey-measured. — [blog.youtube](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/)
- [STUDY] Loewenstein (1994), "The Psychology of Curiosity: A Review and Reinterpretation," *Psychological Bulletin*: curiosity arises "when attention becomes focused on a gap in one's knowledge." Needs some prior knowledge (total ignorance does not trigger it) and a gap that feels closeable; too big or too small reduces curiosity. — [Information Gap Theory summary](https://concepts.dsebastien.net/concept/information-gap-theory/), [Nautilus essay](https://lede-v2.nautil.us/curiosity-depends-on-what-you-already-know-235803)
- [PRACTITIONER, secondary] Muller's "legit bait" (enticing, accurate) vs "click traps" (misleading); "anti-clickbait" = question in title answered in thumbnail. — [fbpe summary](https://old.fbpe.org/clickbait-is-unreasonably-effective.html)
- [BG] Thumbnail text norms among educators: ≤3–4 words, should *add* to the title instead of repeating it, readable at mobile size, one focal subject/face with clear emotion, high contrast. Widely repeated; no official YouTube word limit found.
- [BG] Titles: keep the key hook in the first ~50–60 characters because longer titles are truncated on many surfaces.

### Inferences
- Packaging rubric for the skill: (1) **Promise clarity** – one sentence promise; (2) **Gap** – a specific question the viewer can name; (3) **Closeable** – viewer believes the video answers it; (4) **Truth** – video delivers it (legit bait vs click trap); (5) **Title–thumbnail complementarity** – thumbnail text ≠ title words; (6) **Intro confirms promise within ~5–15s**.
- Title formula library (practitioner-backed): time/tier comparisons, X vs Y, superlative, "I tried X for N days", specific list, counterintuitive claim, personal stake ("Why I quit…"). Each must map to a payoff moment in the script.
- For personal brands, over-extreme packaging risks the satisfaction signals ("Don't recommend channel", low survey ratings) and brand trust. This is inference from the official signal list, not a measured effect.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study found specifically on YouTube clickbait titles vs satisfaction/retention; Loewenstein is general curiosity theory.
- No official YouTube guidance found on thumbnail text length.

## 5. Script formats and words-per-minute

### Takeaway
No authoritative standard. ~150 wpm is a planning convention (about 1,200 words for 8 min, 4,500 for 30 min); documentary narration runs slower (~110–140), commentary faster (~160–190). Script format (full script vs bullets, A-roll/B-roll columns) is creator preference.

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTITIONER] Voiceover industry rule of thumb: ~150 wpm, varies with material and pacing. — [ProZ voiceover forum](https://www2.proz.com/forum/voiceover/194447-voiceover_output_per_hour.html)
- [PRACTITIONER, vendor tool] Documentary narration ~110–140 wpm; explainer/corporate ~140–160 wpm. — [IRPR voiceover calculator](https://sounddesign.irpr.agency/tools/voiceover-word-count-calculator/)
- [PRACTITIONER] ~150 wpm for US English conversation, attributed (secondhand) to the National Center for Voice and Speech. — [VirtualSpeech](https://virtualspeech.com/blog/average-speaking-rate-words-per-)
- [PRACTITIONER, vendor blog 2026] Commentary-style YouTube ~160–190 wpm. — [FlowShorts](https://flowshorts.app/blog/words-per-minute-speaking)
- [PRACTITIONER] MrBeast doc stresses simplicity/clarity so mass audiences understand. — [Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- [BG] Common formats: (a) full word-for-word script read from a teleprompter (documentary/explainer: Johnny Harris-style, Veritasium-style VO); (b) bullet outline with key lines scripted (hook, transitions, ending) for talking-head/vlog; (c) two-column AV script: left = A-roll (on-camera/VO lines), right = B-roll/graphics/SFX/on-screen text; (d) shot list for vlogs (Neistat-style) with story written after filming in the edit.
- [BG] Vietnamese speaking rate is usually counted in syllables (âm tiết), not words; no sourced Vietnamese wpm benchmark found.

### Inferences
- Script skill should output a length estimate: words ÷ 150 (adjust 130 for documentary, 170 for energetic). Script the hook, every re-hook, transitions and ending word-for-word even when the rest is bullets.
- Use an AV two-column (or inline `[B-ROLL: …]`, `[TEXT: …]`) format so the skill's output is editable by the creator.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed YouTube-specific wpm data; no Vietnamese syllables-per-minute benchmark for YouTube narration.

## 6. Vietnamese YouTube context

### Takeaway
YouTube reaches ~62 million people in Vietnam (61% of population, ~72.5% of internet users, late 2025). Storytelling/travel/documentary creators like Khoai Lang Thang show demand for slow, people-focused narrative. Current subscriber counts for named creators need manual checking.

### Cited Findings
- [STUDY/data org] DataReportal Digital 2026 Vietnam (Kepios, data Oct/late 2025): YouTube ad reach 62.1 million = 61.0% of total population, 72.5% of internet users; −200K (−0.3%) YoY, +500K (+0.8%) QoQ Jul→Oct 2025; adult ad audience 48.7% female / 51.3% male. Internet users 85.6M (84.2%); social media user identities 79.0M (77.6%). Report warns ad-reach declines may reflect account corrections, and Google updates reach only every 3–6 months. — [DataReportal: Digital 2026: Vietnam](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-vietnam)

## 7. Common mistakes

### Takeaway
The mistakes with the strongest backing are: packaging that doesn't match the video (official + MrBeast), slow or branded intros (official 2011 + MrBeast first-minute data), weak middles/no re-engagement (MrBeast 3- and 6-minute rules + official dip guidance), signalling the end / weak payoff (MrBeast), and over-investing in production versus packaging (Galloway).

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] Mismatched title/thumbnail vs content → low intro retention; fix by aligning packaging. — [YouTube Help 9314415](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)
- [OFFICIAL, 2011] Flashy branded intros before content. — [blog.youtube](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-creator-playbook-tips-first-15)
- [OFFICIAL] Best moments buried late in the video ("move compelling content earlier"). — [YouTube Help 9314415](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)
- [PRACTITIONER] Telling instead of showing after the first minute; no re-engagement at ~3/6 min; weak back half; signalling the end; non-abrupt endings; jarring sponsor segments ("crater"). — [MrBeast doc via Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- [PRACTITIONER] "And then" story beats instead of "but/therefore". — [The Hustle](https://thehustle.co/storytelling-secrets-netted-matt-stone-trey-parker-600-million)
- [PRACTITIONER] Spending ~95% of effort on filming/editing and too little on ideation/packaging; abandoning formats that still work. — [Colin & Samir](https://www.colinandsamir.com/resources/the-new-rules-of-youtube-from-paddy-galloway)
- [OFFICIAL, 2021] Optimizing for clicks alone: clicks don't mean watching; regret and dislikes are negative signals. — [blog.youtube](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/)
- [STUDY] Curiosity gaps that are too vague (no prior knowledge) or not closeable fail to drive curiosity. — [Information Gap Theory](https://concepts.dsebastien.net/concept/information-gap-theory/)

### Inferences
- Draft-scoring checklist (0–2 each): promise match (title/thumb ↔ first 15s); time-to-value (<45–60s); open loops planted and closed; re-hook cadence (~every 2–4 min, explicit at ~3 and ~6 min); but/therefore transitions; escalating stakes; payoff delivered as promised; no early end-signal; sponsor integration; clarity (one idea per segment, simple language); personal stake/brand voice present but brief.
- After publishing: map dips in the key-moments report to script beats, rewrite those beat types next time; top moments → seeds for new videos (official advice).

### Gaps
- No controlled data quantifying the effect size of each mistake for 8–30 min personal-brand videos; MrBeast data is from a different format and scale.
