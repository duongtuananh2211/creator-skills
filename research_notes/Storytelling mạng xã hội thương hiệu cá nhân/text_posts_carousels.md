# Storytelling in text posts and carousels for personal-brand creators (Facebook, Threads, LinkedIn, X, Instagram, TikTok photo mode)

Research date: 2026-10-29. Tags: [OFFICIAL] = platform's own words/docs/code; [STUDY] = data or independent research; [PRACTITIONER] = marketers, vendors, tool makers, journalists (vendor numbers are self-reported unless noted); [BG] = researcher background knowledge, not checked in this session; verify before you rely on it.

## 1. What platforms officially say about ranking, quality and engagement bait

### Takeaway
All four Meta/LinkedIn/X sources reward real conversation and time spent, and punish explicit asks for engagement. Meta has a written definition of engagement bait ("explicitly request engagement… for purposes other than a specific call to action"). In March 2026 LinkedIn named "comment to agree" prompts as something it now reduces. X's open-source weights (2023) put replies, especially replies the author answers, far above likes. Instagram (Mosseri, Jan 2025) names watch time, likes and sends as its top signals. Sends matter most for reaching non-followers.

### Cited Findings

**Meta / Facebook**
- [OFFICIAL] Meta definition of engagement bait (Content Distribution Guidelines): "Posts that explicitly request engagement (such as votes, shares, comments, tags, likes, or other reactions) for purposes other than a specific call to action (such as seeking help to find missing people or property, raising money, or sharing a petition) on Facebook." Exemption: "this does not include posts that ask people to engage in order to show they do or do not support an issue, or to share time-sensitive information regarding natural disasters and life-threatening events." Rationale: "Feedback shows people dislike spammy posts on Facebook that ask them to interact by liking, sharing, commenting, and taking other actions on posts." — [Meta Transparency Center: Engagement Bait](https://transparency.meta.com/features/approach-to-ranking/content-distribution-guidelines/engagement-bait/)
- [OFFICIAL] Original 2017 announcement: engagement bait "seeks to take advantage of our News Feed algorithm by boosting engagement in order to get greater reach." Example given: "LIKE this if you're an Aries!" Meta said it would begin "demoting individual posts from people and Pages that use engagement bait" and "Pages that repeatedly share engagement bait posts will see more significant drops in reach." Later updates extended this to comments containing engagement bait and to engagement bait spoken in video audio. Exempt: "Posts that ask people for help, advice, or recommendations, such as circulating a missing child report, raising money for a cause, or asking for travel tips." — [Meta Newsroom, "Fighting Engagement Bait on Facebook" (Dec 2017; older guidance, still referenced)](https://about.fb.com/news/2017/12/news-feed-fyi-fighting-engagement-bait-on-facebook/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Coverage of the 2017 policy lists five sub-types: vote baiting, react baiting, share baiting, tag baiting, comment baiting. The current Transparency Center page does not list these sub-types. — [CNN Money (2017)](https://money.cnn.com/2017/12/18/technology/facebook-spam-posts/index.html)
- [OFFICIAL] Meta's "Types of content we demote" lists "Engagement Bait" and "Clickbait Links" under "Responding to People's Direct Feedback". It also says Meta may reduce distribution of "borderline" content. — [Meta Transparency Center: Types of content we demote](https://transparency.meta.com/features/approach-to-ranking/types-of-content-we-demote/)
- [OFFICIAL] Spam policy forbids "requiring or claiming that users must like or share content before they can view or interact with the promised content." This matters for "comment 'X' and I'll send you the file" gating. — [Meta Community Standards: Spam](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam/)
- [OFFICIAL] Demotion strength can depend on "the number of times the poster or commenter has violated our rules previously." — [Meta: Reducing the distribution of problematic content](https://transparency.meta.com/enforcement/taking-action/lowering-distribution-of-problematic-content/)
- [PRACTITIONER] A 2021 fact-check found the viral claim that commenting "Yes" boosts reach was false, and noted that engagement baiting "could even backfire". — [Lead Stories (2021)](https://leadstories.com/hoax-alert/2021/03/fact-check-instagram-and-facebook-did-not-change-their-news-feed-ranking-so-that-no-more-than-7-percent-of-friends-see-posts.html)

**LinkedIn**
- [OFFICIAL] LinkedIn, 12 Mar 2026: "We're improving our systems to reduce repetitive, low‑substance posts and engagement bait, such as 'comment to agree' prompts or videos that don't match the text." "We're reducing recycled and click‑driven posts to make the Feed feel more useful." "You'll see more posts that offer genuine insight, actionable ideas, and thoughtful perspectives." "We want the Feed to be relevant to your interests, not a popularity contest." Recommendations reach people "whether they come from your network or professionals you haven't met yet." — [LinkedIn News, "How LinkedIn Is Improving the Feed…" (Mar 12, 2026)](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/ImprovingTheFeed)
- [OFFICIAL] LinkedIn Engineering (2020) on dwell time: "Click and viral actions can be rare, especially for passive consumers of the feed." "Clicks are noisy indicators of engagement." LinkedIn added a P(skip) model and reduces "the score of all updates by an amount proportional to the predicted P(skip) value." It measures dwell "on the feed" and "after the click". Principle: "time well spent is better than more time spent." — [LinkedIn Engineering, "Understanding dwell time to improve LinkedIn feed ranking" (May 2020)](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/feed/understanding-feed-dwell-time)
- [OFFICIAL] LinkedIn (2019): the feed uses the framework "People You Know, Talking About Things You Care About". "Despite the rumors, the algorithm doesn't favor any particular format." (older guidance) — [LinkedIn Newsroom (Jan 2019)](https://news.linkedin.com/2019/January/what-s-in-your-linkedin-feed--people-you-know--talking-about-thi)
- [OFFICIAL] The feed's algorithms "filter out low-quality and unsafe content". Users can choose "I don't want to see this". — [LinkedIn Blog, "Mythbusting the Feed" (Aug 2022)](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/member/product/mythbusting-the-feed-how-the-algorithm-works)
- [PRACTITIONER] Secondary report: LinkedIn's Professional Community Policies ask members to "create original, professional, relevant, and interesting content" and not to "artificially increase engagement" (pods, pre-agreed likes). The original policy text was not verified. — [ContentIn glossary](https://contentin.io/glossary/engagement-bait/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Reports (via Engadget, attributed to VP Product Laura Lorenzetti) say flagged engagement-bait or AI-styled posts drop out of recommendations to non-connections but stay visible to connections and followers. Not verified at the primary source. — [TechNewsWorld](https://www.technewsworld.com/?p=180344); [ChannelNews](https://www.channelnews.com.au/linkedin-moves-to-reduce-ai-generated-spam-across-user-feeds/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Earlier (2023) LinkedIn changes reportedly reduced polls and engagement bait. Coverage said LinkedIn would show only "helpful and relevant" polls. The page returned 403, so this was not verified. — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/linkedin-feed-less-polls-engagement-bait-384986)
- [PRACTITIONER] "Knowledge and advice" boosting is widely repeated in vendor blogs, but I found no official LinkedIn wording in this session (see Gaps). — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/linkedin-algorithm/)

**Instagram / Threads (Mosseri)**
- [OFFICIAL, reported] Mosseri, Jan 2025: the top three ranking signals are watch time, likes and sends. "Likes are slightly more important for connected content, and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content." Suggested metrics: likes per reach and sends per reach. — [Social Media Today (2025)](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)
- [OFFICIAL, reported] Mosseri, Jan 2025: Instagram will prioritise original content. — [NBC/CNBC](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/instagram-head-mosseri-says-app-will-prioritize-original-content-with-potential-tiktok-ban-days-away/6105247/)
- [OFFICIAL, reported] Mosseri, Oct 2024, on carousels: "multiple pieces of media are going to mean more interactions with your carousel posts, and more interactions is going to mean more reach on average". Also: "if someone sees your carousel post but they don't swipe, we'll often give that carousel a second chance and automatically move to that second piece of media for the viewer." Carousels with music can appear in the Reels tab. — [Social Media Today (Oct 2024)](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/ig-chief-recommends-posting-carousels-improve-reach/730232/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Threads, Nov 2024: Mosseri reportedly said content from followed accounts would get more weight in ranking. — [OnlineMarketing.de](https://onlinemarketing.de/social-media-marketing/neue-ranking-regeln-threads)

**X**
- [OFFICIAL, code] The open-source heavy-ranker README (weights "as of April 5, 2023", which "can be adjusted at any time") gives: like 0.5; retweet 1.0; reply 13.5; good profile click 12.0; good click 11.0; good click v2 (2+ min in conversation) 10.0; video playback50 0.005; **reply engaged by author 75.0**; negative feedback (show less, block, mute) −74.0; report −369.0. — [github.com/twitter/the-algorithm-ml, projects/home/recap/README.md](https://github.com/twitter/the-algorithm-ml/blob/main/projects/home/recap/README.md)
- [PRACTITIONER] X's September 2025 ranking update (Grok-based components) came without a new weight table, so the 2023 numbers may be out of date. — [blckalpaca analysis](https://blckalpaca.at/en/knowledge-base/social-media/social-media-algorithms-distribution/x-twitter-algorithm-open-source)

### Inferences
- A scoring rubric should treat any line that asks readers to "like/comment/share/tag/type X if…" without a real purpose as a high-severity flag on Facebook and LinkedIn. Honest questions that ask for advice or opinions are allowed (Meta exempts "help, advice, or recommendations").
- Good alternatives to bait: end with a specific, low-effort, genuine question tied to the story ("Bạn từng gặp tình huống tương tự chưa, bạn xử lý thế nào?"). Then reply to comments yourself. On X, an author reply is weighted 75 vs a like at 0.5. On LinkedIn, real comment threads add dwell time.
- Dwell time is the hidden currency of text storytelling. A hook that wins the "see more" click, plus a body that holds attention, lines up with LinkedIn's P(skip)/dwell models. The 2020 post says dwell is measured "on the feed", which includes reading expanded text.
- On Instagram, a carousel's slide 2 must work as a second hook, because the "second chance" re-serve starts there.
- Sends (DM shares) matter most for reaching non-followers on Instagram. So "send this to a friend who…" is the natural CTA there, as long as it reads as a real recommendation and not a forced ask. [BG] Meta's written engagement-bait definition includes "shares", so keep it soft.

### Gaps
- No official LinkedIn wording found for "knowledge and advice" prioritisation, dwell-time weighting of long text posts, or document/carousel favouritism. The quote widely attributed to LinkedIn staff (2023–2024) could not be verified.
- No official Threads ranking document found. Threads ranking signals beyond Mosseri's comments are unverified.
- Meta's live page did not show sub-type examples (comment/tag/share baiting) or the date the current wording was set.
- I did not retrieve the Engadget article or a primary LinkedIn source for the "flagged posts lose recommendation reach" mechanism.

## 2. Text length limits and "See more" truncation

### Takeaway
Hard limits are well documented. The "see more" fold is not officially documented on any platform, and varies by device, line breaks and media. Write as if only the first ~1–2 lines (about 100–140 characters) are sure to be seen on mobile.

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTITIONER, approximate] LinkedIn fold: about 210 characters on desktop and about 140 on mobile. Some sources describe it in lines: mobile about 2 lines; desktop about 5 lines for text-only posts and 3 with media. The cutoff "isn't officially documented" and changes with app version and window width. Blank lines use up visible space. — [Medium, A. Shimanski](https://medium.com/@artyom.shimanski/the-linkedin-see-more-cutoff-why-210-characters-decide-your-posts-fate-bc05bf211e0f); [ContentIn](https://contentin.io/blog/linkedin-post-character-limit/)
- [PRACTITIONER] LinkedIn limits: post 3,000 characters; comment 1,250. — [ContentIn](https://contentin.io/glossary/character-limit/)
- [PRACTITIONER, approximate] Facebook fold: about 477 characters on desktop. Mobile estimates conflict (about 125, about 150–200, about 240), and one source says the fold depends on line count (about 3 lines). Link previews and the surface (Page, group, profile) shift the fold. No official figure. — [Blue Compass](https://bluecompass.com/blog/new-character-limits-for-facebook-posts-on-mobile); [TypeCount](https://typecount.com/blog/facebook-post-character-limit-2026); [SocialKit](https://socialk.it/en/tools/facebook-character-counter)
- [PRACTITIONER, approximate] Instagram caption: about the first 125 characters show before "… more", and mobile may cut earlier. The 2,200-character maximum is documented, but the 125 figure comes from guides, not Meta. — [GrowthScribe](https://growthscribe.com/instagram-caption-character-limit/); [Sendible](https://www.sendible.com/insights/instagram-character-limit?hsLang=en)
- [PRACTITIONER, reporting Meta] Threads: 500 characters per post. Since Sept 2025, a free **text attachment** of up to 10,000 characters, opened with "Read more" and written in a blog-style editor. Meta said it was built because users were posting screenshots of longer text. — [TechCrunch (Sept 4, 2025)](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/04/threads-challenges-x-by-offering-free-support-for-up-to-10k-characters-plus-prominent-links); [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/meta-adds-10000-character-text-attachments-to-threads/)
- [PRACTITIONER] X: paid subscribers can post up to 25,000 characters (since 2023). — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/04/threads-challenges-x-by-offering-free-support-for-up-to-10k-characters-plus-prominent-links)

### Inferences
- [BG] X standard (non-Premium) limit is 280 characters (CJK characters count double; Vietnamese Latin script with diacritics counts as normal characters). In the timeline, long Premium posts show about the first 280 characters and then "Show more".
- [BG] Facebook's hard limit for a personal post is about 63,206 characters, so in practice there is no limit for "status dài".
- [BG] TikTok photo-mode descriptions allow up to about 4,000 characters (expanded 2023–2024). Verify.
- Practical rule for skills. **Line 1 must work alone** within about 100–125 characters on every platform. Line 2 should create an open loop before the mobile fold (about 140 LinkedIn / about 125 IG / about 2–3 lines FB). Avoid blank lines and emojis before the hook, because they use up fold space.
- For Threads, split the story into a hook post (500 characters) plus either a reply thread or a 10k text attachment. The hook post must earn the "Read more" tap.

### Gaps
- No official truncation spec on any platform. All fold numbers are approximate, from vendors and tools, and should be presented as ranges.
- Vietnamese diacritics may render wider than average, so the visible character count before the fold on Vietnamese text may be lower. Not measured.

## 3. Post structures for personal storytelling; broetry critique; mobile readability; Vietnamese long-form culture

### Takeaway
The common arc is Hook → context/tension → turning point → lesson → soft CTA. It fits the platforms because the hook wins the fold click and the arc holds dwell time. The main craft risk is the formulaic "broetry" template, now associated with hustle-culture clichés and AI writing, which LinkedIn's 2026 update explicitly targets ("repetitive, low-substance", "recycled"). Vietnamese Facebook has a strong long-form "status dài" culture, but I found few rigorous sources on it.

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTITIONER] "Broetry" means one sentence per line with blank lines between, a blend of "bro" and "poetry". The term is credited to BuzzFeed's 2017 coverage, and Josh Fechter is often named as the style's popularizer. — [ContentIn: Broetry](https://contentin.io/glossary/broetry/); [InsideHook](https://www.insidehook.com/?p=159921)
- [PRACTITIONER] Hootsuite describes the broetry template: a clickbait hook, an unrealistic conversation, heartstring-tugging, jargon, a life lesson, and a request to like or comment. It notes negative associations with hustle culture. — [Hootsuite glossary: Broetry](https://blog.hootsuite.com/social-media-definitions/broetry/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Critics say broetry exploits "see more" clicks and dwell time and becomes hollow engagement bait when overused. Used sincerely and sparingly, its techniques (strong hook, readable spacing, clear takeaway) still help. — [SocialKit: Broetry](https://socialk.it/en/glossary/broetry)
- [PRACTITIONER] Critique: writers break lines constantly but rarely keep the whole post short. — [Business of Business](https://www.businessofbusiness.com/articles/linkedin-broetry-adam-grant-simon-sinek/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Critics compare the writing to third-grade prose because it feels repetitive. — [Gigazine](https://wbgsv0a.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20220123-why-is-linkedin-so-cringe)
- [PRACTITIONER] A browser extension ("LinkedIn Broetry Filter") exists to hide the style and frames it as AI-generated clutter. This shows reader fatigue. — [Extension Auditor](https://extensionauditor.com/scan/linkedin-broetry-filter-lgkjocjkdfdlhndmbakngkdadncekdbd)
- [OFFICIAL] LinkedIn 2026 is reducing "repetitive, low‑substance posts" and "recycled" posts and wants "genuine insight, actionable ideas, and thoughtful perspectives". — [LinkedIn News (Mar 2026)](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/ImprovingTheFeed)
- [PRACTITIONER] A Facebook status can work like a mini blog post. Core story elements: character, plot, message. Honesty and personal connection build trust. Soft-sell: link the story to the offer without conversion-heavy sales language. — [RSM Connect](https://rsmconnect.com/blog/brand-social-media-presence/); [Tutkit](https://www.tutkit.com/en/text-tutorials/22014-storytelling-in-facebook-marketing-strategies-and-tips); [Tailwind](https://tailwindapp.com/blog?p=7299)
- [PRACTITIONER] Vietnamese LinkedIn example: HR professional Trang Nguyen built about 120k followers, starting around 2016–2017 and posting more from 2021. Favikon's Vietnam ranking lists creators such as Hieu Do (economic and social commentary) and Long Ngoc Pham (EY Vietnam). — [Advertising Vietnam](https://advertisingvietnam.com/article/tu-goc-nhin-cua-nha-tuyen-dung-chien-luoc-giup-ung-vien-nang-cao-gia-tri-ca-nhan-thong-qua-xay-dung-hinh-anh-chuyen-nghiep-tren-linkedin-l27351); [Favikon](https://www.favikon.com/fr/blog/top-linkedin-influencers-vietnam)

### Inferences (structures for the skills, mostly [BG]/[PRACTITIONER] craft)
- **Core story-post skeleton** [BG, widely taught]:
  1. *Hook* (1 line, ≤ about 125 characters). Start in the middle of the scene, a concrete number, a surprising admission, or a tension statement. No greeting, no throat-clearing ("Hôm nay mình muốn chia sẻ…").
  2. *Open loop / stakes* (1–2 lines before the mobile fold). Why this matters, what was at risk.
  3. *Context* (short). Who, where, when. Use specific details, not abstractions.
  4. *Tension / struggle*. Obstacle, wrong attempt, inner conflict. Use a line of dialogue or a sensory detail.
  5. *Turning point*. The moment or insight that changed things.
  6. *Lesson / takeaway*. One idea, stated plainly and tied to the reader's situation. Avoid preachiness and lists of 7 lessons.
  7. *CTA*. A genuine question, an invitation to share an experience, a save/send suggestion, or a pointer to the next post. Never "comment YES".
- **Other formats** [BG]: "Before → after → bridge"; "Mistake I made → what I learned"; "Unpopular opinion + story as proof"; "Behind-the-scenes/numbers post"; "Letter to my younger self"; "Observation → story → principle".
- **Broetry guidance for a scorer**: penalise patterns, not short lines as such. Flags: every sentence on its own line for the whole post; one-word dramatic lines ("Silence." "Then it hit me."); invented dialogue with a stranger or child; a "And that's when I realized…" pivot; a moral that is a generic platitude; ending with an engagement ask. Prefer 1–3 sentence paragraphs with varied rhythm.
- **Mobile readability** [BG]: short paragraphs (1–3 lines on a phone), one idea per paragraph, white space between paragraphs, sparing bullets or emojis as visual anchors. Avoid walls of text over about 6 phone lines without a break. Vietnamese sentences tend to be longer, so split compound sentences joined by "và/nhưng/thì".
- **Vietnamese "status dài" culture** [BG]: Vietnamese Facebook has a long tradition of personal-essay posts (reflective essays, "tâm sự", career and parenting stories, travel journals), often 500–2,000+ words, read by followers who expect a personal voice rather than a marketing tone. Well-known long-form Facebook writers/public intellectuals have included figures such as Giáo sư Ngô Bảo Châu (occasional essays), writer Nguyễn Ngọc Tư (literary), doctors, educators and business founders. These names are not verified as current examples in this session, so the report writer should not cite specific individuals without checking. Readers tolerate long text if the voice is intimate and specific ("mình/tôi", direct address "bạn"), but punish "dạy đời" (preachiness) and "flex" (bragging) without vulnerability.
- Vietnamese LinkedIn is smaller (about 10M members) and more English/bilingual and career-focused. Story posts there skew toward career lessons, HR/recruiting, and leadership.

### Gaps
- No academic or official study found on line length or one-sentence-per-line readability for social posts. Readability claims are [BG].
- No Brands Vietnam / Advertising Vietnam article found specifically on "status dài" storytelling norms or named Vietnamese Facebook long-form writers. The search returned only generic or technical results. Needs a targeted Vietnamese-language search.
- No data found comparing short vs long text-post performance in 2025–2026 from a non-vendor source.

## 4. Carousels (Instagram, LinkedIn document posts, TikTok photo mode)

### Takeaway
Instagram officially allows 20 slides (since Aug 2024). Mosseri says carousels earn more reach through extra interactions and a "second chance" re-serve that opens on slide 2. Instagram's key signals (sends, likes; saves are widely cited) favour reference-style, shareable carousels. Norms like "slide 1 = hook, one idea per slide, last slide = CTA/save" are practitioner consensus, not official rules.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL, reported] Instagram raised the carousel limit from 10 to 20 photos/videos (Aug 2024, after testing 15 earlier in the year). — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2833839); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-expands-carousels-to-20-frames/723792/)
- [OFFICIAL, reported] Mosseri: carousels get more reach because more media means more interactions. Unswiped carousels get a "second chance" that auto-advances to slide 2. Carousels with music can show in the Reels tab. — [Social Media Today (Oct 2024)](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/ig-chief-recommends-posting-carousels-improve-reach/730232/); [AGI](https://www.agi.it/innovazione/news/2024-10-18/importanza-caroselli-strategia-instagram-visibilita-28331479/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Inference widely drawn from Mosseri's statement: make slide 2 a strong second hook. (Author inference, not Mosseri's words.) — [Lia Haberman, ICYMI](https://liahaberman.substack.com/p/icymi-carousels-are-back-baby?open=false)
- [PRACTITIONER] Instagram tested like counts for individual carousel frames. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-tests-like-counts-for-frames-in-a-carousel/753516/)
- [OFFICIAL, reported] Sends are the stronger signal for non-follower reach; likes for follower reach. — [Social Media Today (2025)](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)
- [PRACTITIONER] LinkedIn carousels/documents are often cited as dwell-time drivers ("each swipe counts as continued reading"). LinkedIn has not confirmed this. — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/linkedin-algorithm/); [AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm)
- [OFFICIAL] LinkedIn (2019): "the algorithm doesn't favor any particular format." (Old, but the only official format statement found.) — [LinkedIn Newsroom](https://news.linkedin.com/2019/January/what-s-in-your-linkedin-feed--people-you-know--talking-about-thi)

### Inferences (carousel craft, mostly [BG]/[PRACTITIONER])
- **Slide count** [BG]: personal-brand educational or story carousels usually run 6–10 slides. Use 10–15 only when every slide earns its place. 20 is the cap, not the target. LinkedIn document posts usually 6–12 pages.
- **Slide 1 (cover/hook)** [BG]: one bold promise or tension (≤ about 8–12 words), large type, high contrast, readable as a thumbnail. Optional face or personal photo for personal brand. Signal a payoff ("7 năm làm freelance, 3 sai lầm đắt nhất").
- **Slide 2 (second hook)** [inferred from Mosseri]: must stand alone for viewers re-served from slide 2. Restate the stakes or open the story.
- **Body slides** [BG]: one idea per slide. About 20–40 words max on IG and up to about 50 on LinkedIn PDF. Same visual template on every slide; a progress cue (1/8) or "→" swipe cue; a narrative arc across slides (setup → conflict → turns → resolution) or a list arc with a story frame.
- **Last slide** [BG]: recap plus one CTA (save for later / send to someone / follow for part 2 / answer a specific question). One CTA, not three. Plus caption that adds context rather than repeating slides.
- **Story carousel arc** [BG]: Slide 1 hook → 2 scene/stakes → 3–4 struggle → 5 turning point → 6–7 lesson(s) → 8 reader application → 9 CTA.
- **TikTok photo mode** [BG]: up to 35 images, auto-play with music, swipeable. Text overlays must be very short (phone-screen-safe zones). Caption carries the story. Verify current limits.
- **LinkedIn document post** [BG]: upload a PDF (up to about 300 pages / 100 MB). Square (1080×1080) or portrait (1080×1350) pages read best on mobile. Add a title, since LinkedIn shows the document title on the post. Verify current specs.
- Design for "saves" and "sends": checklists, frameworks, before/after, scripts, and "share with a colleague who…" value make carousels reference-worthy. Pure narrative carousels need a takeaway slide worth saving.

### Gaps
- No official Instagram or LinkedIn statement found on ideal slide count, text density, or save-weighting in 2025–2026. "Saves" as a ranking signal is widely asserted by practitioners but was not found in Mosseri's 2025 top-3 list (watch time, likes, sends).
- TikTok photo-mode official limits and ranking guidance were not retrieved this session.
- LinkedIn's current document-post specs (page and size limits) were not verified this session.

## 5. Copywriting craft: specificity, sensory detail, dialogue, paragraphs, emoji, hashtags

### Takeaway
Hashtags are no longer a reach lever. Instagram capped them at 5 per post/reel (Dec 2025), Threads uses one topic tag, and Mosseri has long downplayed them. Craft value comes from specificity, scene, real dialogue and a distinct voice, which also aligns with LinkedIn's 2026 anti-"recycled" stance and Instagram's originality push.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL, reported] Instagram now caps hashtags at **5 per post or reel** (previously 30), after about a year of tests (some users were limited to 3). The stated reason is relevance and less spam. Instagram advises against generic tags such as #reels and #explore. — [Social Media Today (Dec 2025)](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/); [Blog du Modérateur](https://www.blogdumoderateur.com/instagram-limite-hashtags-5-par-publication/); [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/instagram-hashtags-five-per-post-10923075). Conflicting: one blogger says the cap applies only to test groups or schedulers — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/why-instagram-only-lets-you-use-5-hashtags-some-posts/) (weighted lower).
- [PRACTITIONER] Instagram ended hashtag following (announced Nov 2024). Mosseri has advised against using hashtags as a reach tactic since 2022. — [blckalpaca](https://blckalpaca.at/de/wissen/social-media/social-media-algorithmen-distribution/instagram-algorithmus-2026)
- [OFFICIAL, reported] Threads uses **topic tags**: one per post, no "#" shown, spaces allowed. Mosseri said the design aims at communities, "less on engagement hacking". One source says some accounts can now add a few more tags, which is unconfirmed. Tapping a tag runs a search that also surfaces untagged posts containing the phrase. — [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2023/12/08/threads-now-has-tags-but-youre-limited-to-one-per-post/); [MarTech](https://martech.org/marketers-can-now-use-topic-tags-on-threads-to-boost-engagement/); [Social News Desk](https://www.socialnewsdesk.com/blog/threads-topic-tagging-how-to-use-this-brand-new-feature); [Postiz](https://postiz.com/tools/threads-hashtag-tool)
- [OFFICIAL] LinkedIn wants "genuine insight, actionable ideas, and thoughtful perspectives" and less "recycled" content. — [LinkedIn News (Mar 2026)](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/ImprovingTheFeed)
- [OFFICIAL, reported] Instagram is prioritising original content (2025). — [NBC/CNBC](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/instagram-head-mosseri-says-app-will-prioritize-original-content-with-potential-tiktok-ban-days-away/6105247/)

### Inferences (craft rules for writer and scorer skills, [BG] unless noted)
- **Specificity**: concrete numbers, dates, places, names (with permission), amounts in VND, exact quotes. "Tháng 3/2021, tài khoản còn 2,3 triệu" beats "Có thời điểm mình rất khó khăn". A scorer can count concrete nouns and numbers per 100 words.
- **Sensory detail**: 1–2 sensory details at the scene's peak (sound, temperature, what was on the desk). Don't overwrite.
- **Dialogue**: short, real-sounding lines that move the tension. Flag implausible "a stranger/child taught me a lesson" dialogue (a broetry cliché per Hootsuite).
- **Paragraphing**: 1–3 sentences per paragraph and varied rhythm. No full-post one-line-per-sentence.
- **Emoji**: functional only (section markers, light tone). 0–3 in a personal story. No emoji in the hook of a serious story. Avoid emoji walls, which read as marketing or AI.
- **Hashtags**: Instagram 3–5 specific tags (cap 5); Threads 1 topic tag; LinkedIn 0–3 [BG; no official LinkedIn guidance found this session]; Facebook 0–2 [BG]; X 0–1 [BG]. Place at the end and never in the hook.
- **Links**: [PRACTITIONER] native content is widely believed to be favoured over outbound links on LinkedIn and others. Put links in the first comment or on the last slide, unverified — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/linkedin-algorithm/). Threads explicitly lets the main post carry a link alongside a text attachment — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/04/threads-challenges-x-by-offering-free-support-for-up-to-10k-characters-plus-prominent-links).
- **Voice and AI tells**: avoid AI-sounding clichés (Vietnamese equivalents of "In today's fast-paced world": "Trong thời đại 4.0…", "Hãy cùng mình khám phá…"), forced triads, and "not X but Y" reversals in every paragraph. LinkedIn's 2026 update and reader fatigue (the broetry filter extension) both push against templated voice.
- **Video/text match**: if a text post carries a video or image, the media must match the text. LinkedIn explicitly names "videos that don't match the text" as engagement bait.

### Gaps
- No official LinkedIn or Facebook 2025–2026 hashtag guidance retrieved.
- No independent study found on emoji use and engagement for storytelling posts.

### Takeaway

Instagram (11.7M), LinkedIn (10.0M members, +16.3%), X (7.3M) and Threads (4.65M) are niche but useful for professional/urban audiences. Default skill targeting: Facebook first; LinkedIn for career/B2B; Threads as an emerging conversational channel.

## 7. Common mistakes (consolidated checklist for a draft scorer)

### Takeaway
Most failures are first-line failures (no hook before the fold), engagement bait, generic and unspecific writing, templated broetry/AI voice, and carousels with too much text or no arc. Several are explicitly penalised by platforms.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] Explicit asks to like/comment/share/tag/vote without a real purpose are demoted on Facebook, and repeat offenders lose more reach. — [Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/features/approach-to-ranking/content-distribution-guidelines/engagement-bait/); [Meta Newsroom 2017](https://about.fb.com/news/2017/12/news-feed-fyi-fighting-engagement-bait-on-facebook/)
- [OFFICIAL] "Comment to agree" prompts, repetitive/low-substance and recycled posts, and media that doesn't match the text are reduced on LinkedIn. — [LinkedIn News (Mar 2026)](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/ImprovingTheFeed)
- [OFFICIAL] Gating content behind likes or shares ("like/share to see…") violates Meta's spam policy. — [Meta: Spam](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam/)
- [OFFICIAL] Clickbait that sets misleading expectations ("withholding information or using sensationalist phrasing") is demoted. — [Meta: Clickbait Links](https://transparency.meta.com/features/approach-to-ranking/content-distribution-guidelines/clickbait-links/)
- [OFFICIAL, code] On X, negative feedback (−74) and reports (−369) far outweigh likes (0.5), so provocation that triggers "show less" or mute is costly. — [twitter/the-algorithm-ml README](https://github.com/twitter/the-algorithm-ml/blob/main/projects/home/recap/README.md)
- [PRACTITIONER] Broetry clichés: clickbait hook, unrealistic dialogue, heartstring-tugging, jargon, life lesson, like/comment request. — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/social-media-definitions/broetry/)
- [OFFICIAL, reported] Hashtag stuffing is pointless on Instagram (cap 5) and generic tags don't help. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/)

### Inferences (scorer checklist; [BG] unless tagged above)
1. Hook buried: greeting, context or emoji before the tension; first line over about 125 characters; first 2 lines give the whole point away (no open loop).
2. Clickbait hook that the body doesn't pay off (Meta clickbait rules; erodes trust).
3. Engagement bait CTA ("comment YES", "tag 3 friends", "share if you agree", "comment 'X' to get the file").
4. Generic and abstract: no numbers, names, places or dialogue; lesson as a platitude ("hãy luôn cố gắng").
5. Broetry overload / AI voice: one-line paragraphs throughout, dramatic one-word lines, fake dialogue, "And then I realized", triads, the same template every post.
6. Humble-brag without vulnerability. Vietnamese audiences call it "flex"/"dạy đời" [BG].
7. Too many lessons. One post, one takeaway.
8. Wall of text with no paragraph breaks, or paragraphs over about 4 phone lines.
9. Hashtag/emoji stuffing; hashtags in the hook.
10. Link in body on LinkedIn/Facebook when a link-in-comment is possible [PRACTITIONER, unverified]; media that doesn't match the text (LinkedIn official).
11. Carousel: weak cover, slide 2 not standalone, more than one idea per slide, tiny text, no arc, no final CTA, caption duplicating slides.
12. Author disappears after posting. Not replying to comments forfeits the strongest X signal (reply engaged by author = 75) and conversation depth on LinkedIn and Facebook.
13. Cross-posting the identical text everywhere without adapting to limits (Threads 500, X 280, LinkedIn 3,000) and register.

### Gaps
- No platform publishes a "common mistakes" list for personal storytelling. Items 1–2 and 4–13 (except where tagged) are craft consensus [BG/PRACTITIONER] and should be presented as heuristics, not rules.
