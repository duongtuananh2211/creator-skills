## Q1. Hook taxonomy: types, examples, evidence level

### Takeaway
Large randomized headline datasets (Upworthy, 32k+ A/B tests) support a few hook levers: negative or loss-framed emotion, more specific and longer headlines, and personal "I/he/she" framing rather than "we". They also show that any single feature has only small predictive power. Most named hook types (curiosity gap, contrarian, in medias res, "how I…") are practitioner lore built on solid psychology (Loewenstein's information gap) but rarely tested as categories. The skill should label each hook with an evidence level and push the user to A/B test, not promise results.

### Cited Findings

**Evidence base (headline-level research)**
- [STUDY] The Upworthy Research Archive (Matias, Munger, Le Quéré, Ebersole; *Scientific Data*, 2021) covers 32,487 A/B experiments, 150,817 arms and 538,272,878 participant assignments (Jan 2013 to Apr 2015). Each test varied a headline plus image "package". It is open on OSF (osf.io/jd64p) — [PMC8329003](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8329003); [Archive site](https://upworthy.natematias.com/)
- [STUDY] Caveat: tests from 25 Jun 2013 to 10 Jan 2014 had likely randomization problems. A June 2024 update found systematic randomization issues in 22% of tests, but re-checks with the authors of six peer-reviewed studies found no prior findings materially affected — [Upworthy archive 2024 update](https://upworthy.natematias.com/2024-06-upworthy-archive-update.html); [Author Correction PMC11239857](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11239857/)
- [STUDY] Robertson, Pröllochs, Schwarzenegger, Pärnamets, Van Bavel & Feuerriegel, "Negativity drives online news consumption", *Nature Human Behaviour* 7: 812–822 (2023), Registered Report. Upworthy data: about 105k headline variations, about 5.7M clicks, more than 370M impressions. Negative words raised CTR and positive words lowered it. Each extra negative word raised CTR by about 2.3% on average — [PMC10202797](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10202797/); [PsyPost summary](https://www.psypost.org/a-particular-type-of-language-boosts-online-news-consumption-study-suggests/); DOI 10.1038/s41562-023-01538-4
- [STUDY] Same paper, emotion breakdown: sadness words had a significant positive effect on clicks. Nieman Lab headline: "sad words are more effective than angry or scary ones" — [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2023/03/negative-words-in-news-headlines-generate-more-clicks-but-sad-words-are-more-effective-than-angry-or-scary-ones/); [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/a-particular-type-of-language-boosts-online-news-consumption-study-suggests/)
- [STUDY] Gligorić, Lifchits, West & Anderson, "Linguistic effects on news headline success" (*PLoS One*, 2023, Registered Report, 24,333 Upworthy headline pairs):
  - Best model predicted the winning headline only 54.42% of the time (chance is 50%), peaking at 58.3% when the CTR gap was large. The authors call it "a genuinely hard prediction problem".
  - Longer headlines won more (β=0.07). Readability had no effect.
  - Negative-emotion words helped (β=0.18). Positive words had no significant effect.
  - First-person singular "I" (β=0.24) and third-person singular "he/she" (β=0.22) helped. First-person plural "we" hurt (β=−0.15). Second-person "you" was not significant at the preregistered α (β=0.05, p=0.04).
  - Indefinite articles ("a/an", which signal specificity or novelty) helped (β=0.12).
  - — [PMC10038272](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10038272/)
- [STUDY] Banerjee & Urminsky, "The Language That Drives Engagement: A Systematic Large-scale Analysis of Headline Experiments", *Marketing Science* 44(3): 566–592 (published 2024/2025; sources differ on the year). Key conclusion: textual cues from prior research do affect headline effectiveness, but "prior research does not provide useful guidance as to the direction of the effects". Industry headline advice often has the sign wrong or unstable — [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mksc.2021.0018); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormksc/v44y2025i3p566-592.html); [2021 working paper PDF](https://home.uchicago.edu/ourminsky/Banerjee_Urminsky_Headlines.pdf). The 2021 PDF warns its exploratory results "are likely to differ", so feature-level effects from it are not reported here.
- [STUDY] Kuiken, Schuth, Spitters & Marx, "Effective Headlines of Newspaper Articles in a Digital Environment", *Digital Journalism* 5(10), 2017. 1,638 Blendle newsletter headlines rewritten by editors. "Most, but not all" clickbait features significantly raised clicks, but headline effectiveness "cannot be predicted" from clickbait features alone — [UvA PDF](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/20924776/Effective_Headlines_of_Newspaper_Articles_in_a_Digital_Environment.pdf). I did not retrieve per-feature results (numbers, questions).

**Psychology of curiosity**
- [STUDY] Loewenstein, "The Psychology of Curiosity: A Review and Reinterpretation", *Psychological Bulletin* 116(1): 75–98 (1994). Curiosity is "cognitively induced deprivation that arises from the perception of a gap in knowledge or understanding". It is intense, transient, impulsive and "often disappointing once satisfied". Curiosity needs some prior knowledge first, so the audience must know enough to notice the gap ("prime the pump") — [Stafforini abstract](https://stafforini.com/works/loewenstein-1994-psychology-curiosity-review/); [PBS NOVA](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/article/how-to-stimulate-curiosity/)

### Inferences
- The skill should rank hook options by fit and honesty, not by "proven" CTR. The best large-scale evidence says single features predict winners only slightly better than chance (54–58%) and that feature directions are unstable across contexts. Generating 5–10 labeled variants for the creator to test fits the evidence better than a single "best" hook.
- Evidence-backed levers to prefer: specific detail (a/an, concrete nouns, numbers that are true), first-person singular storytelling, and honest negative or loss framing, especially sadness or difficulty rather than outrage. Avoid "we/chúng ta" brand-voice openers. This is an inference from Upworthy findings, which are US news headlines from 2013–15, so transfer to Vietnamese short-video hooks is unproven.
- Loewenstein's "prime the pump" condition implies a good curiosity hook names a topic the viewer already cares about, then withholds one specific piece. A vague tease ("you won't believe this") gives no prior knowledge to anchor the gap.
- Suggested Vietnamese hook templates for the skill. These are my synthesis [BG]; none come from a verified source:
  - Thú nhận: "Mình đã [sai lầm cụ thể] suốt [thời gian]."
  - Phản biện: "Ai cũng bảo [lời khuyên phổ biến]. Mình làm ngược lại và [kết quả]."
  - Kết quả trước: "[Kết quả có số liệu thật] — đây là [n] việc mình đã làm."
  - Gọi tên: "Nếu bạn đang [tình huống cụ thể], 30 giây này dành cho bạn."
  - Vào cảnh: "[Thời điểm]. [Một câu thoại hoặc hành động căng thẳng]."
  - Cảnh báo: "Đừng [hành động] trước khi [điều kiện]."
- Thumbnail text (for the skill): 2–5 words that complement rather than repeat the title. A common practitioner heuristic is title = promise, thumbnail = emotion or stakes [BG/PRACTITIONER, not verified this session].

- Per-feature findings on numbers and questions from Kuiken 2017 and Banerjee & Urminsky (final version) were not retrieved.
- "Scott 2021" on clickbait trust could not be identified or verified. Possibly confused with another author; not cited.

## Q2. Clickbait, trust, and where hooks cross the line (platform policy + ethics)

### Takeaway
Both Meta and YouTube penalize the gap between promise and delivery and the explicit begging for interactions, not curiosity itself. Studies show exaggeration costs credibility more than question format does. For a personal brand, the line should be: every hook must be literally true, and the content must pay it off early.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube "Spam, deceptive practices & scams policies":
  - Prohibits "Malicious clickbait: Using maliciously misleading titles, thumbnails, descriptions, or imagery to trick users into clicking on a video that does not deliver what was promised." Example: a title offering a full sports match when the video shows only a clip.
  - Also prohibits "misleading viewers to boost content engagement".
  - Consequences: content removal, warning or strike; 3 strikes in 90 days can mean termination; monetization suspension or channel termination for some violations.
  - The policy applies to thumbnails, posts, comments and unlisted/private content.
  - — [YouTube Help 2801973](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973)
- [OFFICIAL/secondary press] In late 2024 YouTube announced stepped-up enforcement against titles and thumbnails that promise what the video does not deliver. It started in India with a focus on breaking news and current events, initially removing content without strikes — [SAN](https://san.com/cc/youtube-cracks-down-on-clickbait-with-new-enforcement-measures/); [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/industry-updates/youtube-crack-down-misleading-clickbait-videos-8556876). Global rollout status as of 2026 is unverified.
- [OFFICIAL] Meta (Dec 2017) demotes individual posts using engagement bait. Types: vote baiting, react baiting, share baiting, tag baiting, comment baiting, e.g. "LIKE this if you're an Aries!". Pages that repeatedly use it get Page-level demotion. Posts genuinely asking for help (missing child, fundraiser, travel tips) are exempt. Detection uses an ML model trained on hundreds of thousands of reviewed posts — [Meta Newsroom: Fighting Engagement Bait](https://about.fb.com/news/2017/12/news-feed-fyi-fighting-engagement-bait-on-facebook/)
- [OFFICIAL] Meta Transparency Center "Types of content we demote" (updated 2 Jul 2025) lists "Engagement Bait" and "Clickbait Links" under "Responding to People's Direct Feedback". Demoted content gets reduced distribution "in Feed for everyone" — [Meta: Types of content we demote](https://transparency.meta.com/features/approach-to-ranking/types-of-content-we-demote/)
- [OFFICIAL] Meta's "remove, reduce, inform" strategy (since 2016) reduces distribution of low-quality content "such as clickbait and engagement bait" that does not violate the Community Standards — [Meta: Lowering distribution of problematic content](https://transparency.meta.com/enforcement/taking-action/lowering-distribution-of-problematic-content)
- [STUDY] Molyneux & Coddington (*Journalism Practice*, 2019/2020, two online experiments): clickbait headlines lowered perceived credibility and quality. The question-style headline was rated lower quality than non-clickbait, but the effect was small — [CHI'21 paper summarizing it](https://pike.psu.edu/publications/chi21.pdf); [Temple ScholarShare](https://scholarshare.temple.edu/entities/publication/0f000984-ccd7-461a-881c-6b8df9539452)
- [STUDY, via secondary summary] Scacco & Muddiman (Engaging News Project): users engaged more with non-clickbait headlines than with either clickbait style tested — [CHI'21 PDF](https://pike.psu.edu/publications/chi21.pdf)
- [STUDY, thesis-level] Exaggerated headlines were rated less credible than non-exaggerated ones, whether phrased as a statement or a question — [Radboud thesis](https://theses.ubn.ru.nl/handle/123456789/14307). A Hogrefe *Journal of Media Psychology* article separates "exaggerated" from "questioning" clickbait and examines effects on learning — [Hogrefe](https://econtent.hogrefe.com/doi/10.1027/1864-1105/a000298)
- [STUDY] Kuiken et al. 2017: clickbait features mostly raise clicks, showing the short-term click vs long-term trust tension — [UvA PDF](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/20924776/Effective_Headlines_of_Newspaper_Articles_in_a_Digital_Environment.pdf)
- [STUDY] Loewenstein 1994: curiosity is "often disappointing once satisfied". Mechanistically, over-promising curiosity hooks set up disappointment — [Stafforini](https://stafforini.com/works/loewenstein-1994-psychology-curiosity-review/)

### Inferences
- Proposed ethical line for the skill, as checks to run on every generated hook:
  1. **Truth check.** Every factual element (numbers, results, timeframes, "I did X") must be literally true for the creator. If unknown, the skill puts in a placeholder [điền số liệu thật] and does not invent one.
  2. **Payoff check.** The promise in the hook or thumbnail is delivered, ideally within the first 20–30% of the video or post. Wording follows YouTube's "does not deliver what was promised".
  3. **No interaction begging as the hook.** "Comment 'YES' if…", "tag a friend who…", "share if you agree" in the opening line falls into Meta's engagement bait categories. A CTA may ask for a comment that adds value (e.g., "Bạn sẽ làm gì trong tình huống này?"), placed at the end.
  4. **Proportional emotion.** Negativity raises clicks, but fear or outrage hooks unrelated to the substance count as exaggeration, which costs credibility. Prefer honest stakes and sadness or difficulty over manufactured outrage.
- Severity labels for hook warnings: 🔴 Vi phạm (misleading or fabricated, engagement bait), 🟡 Rủi ro (exaggeration, vague tease with no anchor), 🟢 OK.

### Gaps
- The specific Meta Content Distribution Guidelines text for "Clickbait Links" and "Engagement Bait" (the current definitions page) was not retrieved; only the category listing (updated Jul 2025) was confirmed.
- TikTok's equivalent policy on misleading content or engagement bait (Community Guidelines / For You eligibility) was not researched in this pass.
- No verified 2026 changes to YouTube's clickbait enforcement scope.

## Q3. Criteria storytelling experts use to judge a story (rubric source material)

### Takeaway
Expert frameworks converge on about 8 criteria: stakes, change or transformation, a single clear point, concrete sensory specifics, causal momentum (but/therefore), scenes over summary at key moments, truth and the teller as protagonist, and audience relevance. The Moth's verified guidance ("A story without stakes is an essay", one key decision, one-sentence focus, scenes vs summary vs reflection) is the most citable checklist source.

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTITIONER, book excerpt] The Moth, *How to Tell a Story* (2022):
  - "A story without stakes is an essay." Ask what you could lose or gain. Stakes may be internal, external, or both.
  - At the core of a story is one key decision.
  - Define the story in one sentence early to choose which details to keep.
  - Three tools: scenes (key moments), summary (moving time along), reflection (what you learned). The climax is almost always a scene.
  - Specific sensory detail makes a story vivid.
  - Moth rules: true stories, teller is the main character, concrete stakes. StorySLAM about 5 min, Main Stage 10–12 min. No full script; storytellers outline in bullets.
  - — [Nieman Storyboard on The Moth](https://niemanstoryboard.org/?p=893); [Goodreads notes, excerpts](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/59000869-how-to-tell-a-story/7552515-erhan); [Brynn Hambley review](https://brynnhambley.substack.com/p/published-work-review-how-to-tell)
- [BG] Matthew Dicks, *Storyworthy* (2018):
  - Every story is about a "five-second moment" of change or realization. Find that moment first, then start the story as far from it as possible (the opposite state) to show transformation.
  - The "dinner test": would you tell it this way at dinner?
  - "Homework for Life" for collecting moments.
  - Stakes tools: elephant (state the need early), backpack (share the plan before it fails), breadcrumbs (hints), hourglass (slow down at the climax), crystal ball (predictions).
  - Avoid opening with the weather or a thesis.
  - Not fetched this session. Verify wording before quoting.
- [BG] Trey Parker & Matt Stone (2011 NYU/mtvU class video): beats joined by "and then" are boring. Beats should be joined by "but" or "therefore" (causality and complication). Widely cited, not fetched.
- [BG] Chip & Dan Heath, *Made to Stick* (2007), SUCCESs: Simple (core message), Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Stories. "Curse of knowledge" as the main enemy. "Unexpected" ties to curiosity gaps; the Heaths explicitly cite Loewenstein's gap theory. Not fetched.
- [BG] Nancy Duarte, *Resonate* (2010) and *DataStory*: a "sparkline" structure alternating "what is" and "what could be", ending with a "new bliss". The audience is the hero, the presenter is the mentor. Useful for audience-relevance criteria. Not fetched.
- [BG] Journalism editing tradition: the "so what?" / nut-graf test (why should this reader care, why now) and "show, don't tell" (concrete scene over abstraction). Not fetched.

### Inferences
**Draft rubric for the review skill.** Score 1–5 per criterion. Criteria marked * are weighted ×2 for stories, less for pure tip content.

| Criterion (VN label) | Question the reviewer asks | Primary source |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook & lời hứa* | Does the first line or 3 seconds create a specific gap the audience cares about, and is it paid off? | Loewenstein [STUDY]; YouTube policy [OFFICIAL] |
| 2. Stakes (được/mất)* | What could the teller lose or gain? Stated early? | The Moth [PRACTITIONER] |

## Q4. Personal-brand storytelling: authenticity research, fabrication risk, story types, core story

### Takeaway
Survey-based studies consistently link perceived influencer authenticity to parasocial interaction and trust, which then drives outcomes. Most are cross-sectional PLS-SEM studies of modest quality. The practical implication is clear: fabricated or exaggerated stories attack the very mechanism (authenticity → trust) personal brands rely on.

### Cited Findings
- [STUDY] Cross-sectional PLS-SEM study, n=195 social media users: perceived authenticity significantly increases parasocial interaction and trust in the influencer, which mediate effects on brand outcomes — [KFUPM eprint](https://eprints.kfupm.edu.sa/id/eprint/144310/). Venue not confirmed.
- [STUDY] *International Journal of Tourism Research* (2022), n=420: parasocial interaction, perceived authenticity and self-congruity each positively affect the influencer's source credibility — [USC Australia record](https://research.usc.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-effects-of-parasocial-interaction-authenticity/99656498502621)
- [STUDY] *Frontiers in Communication* (2026), Greece + UK samples: authenticity and parasocial relationship → trust → sustainable purchase intention. Perceived greenwashing (i.e., perceived inauthenticity) weakens the trust → intention link — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2026.1764803)

### Inferences
- Story types the skill should recognize and tag. These are [BG] practitioner conventions, not research-derived:

  2. Failure/lesson story (thất bại & bài học).
  3. Turning-point / five-second moment (khoảnh khắc thay đổi).
  4. Behind the scenes (hậu trường): process, numbers, tools.
  5. Customer/audience story (câu chuyện khách hàng/người theo dõi), told with consent.
  6. Values/belief story (quan điểm): contrarian stance rooted in experience.

- Core-story consistency check for the skill: does the draft reinforce the creator's stated positioning (who I help, with what, why me)? A one-line "core story" field in the skill's config lets the reviewer flag off-brand drafts. This is [BG] design suggestion.

## Q5. Vulnerability vs oversharing; how editors give actionable feedback (severity levels)

### Takeaway
Brené Brown's "floodlighting" concept offers a citable test: vulnerability shared to bait connection, or before the relationship or healing supports it, backfires. Editorial practice separates structural/developmental edits from line edits and copy edits. The review skill should output issues in that order with severity labels and concrete rewrites.

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTITIONER/book] Brown's "floodlighting": oversharing as a shield against real vulnerability. Painful disclosures to people who have not earned trust lead to listener withdrawal, which the sharer reads as proof nobody cares. "That darkness is lost when we use vulnerability to floodlight our listener… the response is disconnection" (*Daring Greatly*; some sources attribute it to *The Power of Vulnerability*) — [Goodreads quote](https://www.goodreads.com/quotes/10109123-that-darkness-is-lost-when-we-use-vulnerability-to-floodlight); [xoNecole explainer](https://www.xonecole.com/what-is-floodlighting/)
- [BG] Brown also argues "vulnerability without boundaries is not vulnerability". She has said she shares stories publicly only after she has worked through them ("scars, not open wounds" is a common paraphrase, possibly not her wording). Verify before quoting.
- [BG] Editorial hierarchy: developmental/structural edit (premise, structure, stakes, order) → line edit (sentence rhythm, clarity, voice) → copy edit/proofread (grammar, consistency, facts). Editors fix structure before polishing lines, because line edits on a paragraph that will be cut are wasted.

### Inferences
- Vulnerability check for the rubric (criterion 8), using Brown [BG/PRACTITIONER]:
  - (a) Is the struggle resolved enough that the teller can extract a lesson? If not, it is floodlighting risk.
  - (b) Does the disclosure serve the audience's takeaway, or only the teller's catharsis or attention?
  - (c) Are third parties (family, ex-partners, clients) exposed without consent?
  - (d) Does the level of disclosure match the relationship depth with this audience (new followers vs long-time community)?
- Feedback output format for the review skill (synthesized):
  - **Tổng điểm** + 1-line verdict (Đăng được / Sửa nhẹ / Sửa cấu trúc / Viết lại).
  - **🔴 Nghiêm trọng (Critical)**: misleading hook, fabricated or unverifiable claim, engagement bait, floodlighting, privacy breach of third parties, no story point. Must fix before posting.
  - **🟠 Cấu trúc (Structural)**: missing stakes, no transformation, "and then" chain, buried hook, takeaway unclear, wrong order. Each comes with a proposed restructure (e.g., "đưa câu ở đoạn 4 lên làm hook").
  - **🟡 Câu chữ (Line)**: abstractions to replace with specifics, rhythm, filler, "we"-voice openers, weak verbs. Each item quotes the original line and gives a before → after rewrite.
  - **🟢 Giữ lại (What works)**: 1–3 strengths, so the creator keeps them in revisions.
  - Limit to the top 3–5 issues per severity to keep feedback actionable. Always quote the exact span and give a concrete replacement, not just "make it more engaging".
- Order of operations: run the Critical and Structural passes before Line edits. If the structure fails, give the restructured outline instead of polishing lines.

### Gaps
- No published severity taxonomy specific to social-media story editing was found. The 🔴/🟠/🟡/🟢 scheme is synthesized from general editorial practice [BG].
- Brené Brown's exact wording on boundaries ("vulnerability minus boundaries…", "share from scars") was not verified against primary text.
