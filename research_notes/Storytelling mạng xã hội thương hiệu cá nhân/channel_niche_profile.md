## 1. Niche selection frameworks (with accurate attributions)

### Takeaway
The frameworks agree. Start where your competence and interest overlap (Pulizzi's "sweet spot"), then shift ("tilt") into a corner with little competition. Aim to serve a small, specific group very well (Godin's smallest viable audience/market, Kelly's 1,000 true fans). Define that corner against what the viewer would watch instead (Dunford: competitive alternatives → unique attributes → value → who cares → category). Practitioners such as Paddy Galloway add: start in 1–2 sub-niches, then widen.

### Cited Findings
**Joe Pulizzi, *Content Inc.* (2015; 2nd ed. 2021)**
- [PRACTITIONER] Six steps: (1) **Sweet Spot**, "Identify the intersection of your unique competency and your personal passion"; (2) **Content Tilting**, "Determine how you can 'tilt' your sweet spot to find a place where little or no competition exists"; (3) **Building the Base**, "Establish your number-one channel for disseminating content (blog, podcast, YouTube, etc.)"; (4) **Harvesting Audience**, "Use social-media and SEO to convert one-time visitors into long-term subscribers"; (5) Diversification; (6) Monetization. — [Convince & Convert podcast summary](https://www.convinceandconvert.com/podcasts/episodes/6-steps-to-monetize-your-content-marketing/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Pulizzi (Authority Rainmaker talk): the sweet spot is what you know + what you love. He notes there "isn't always a perfect overlap between passion and expertise, but there should be some." — [TopRank Marketing](https://www.toprankmarketing.com/blog/content-marketing-building-audience/)
- [BG] Pulizzi's own example of a tilt: Ann Reardon's "How to Cook That", which tilted baking toward the science behind it and debunking fake food videos. He also says to pick one main platform and ship on a consistent schedule before diversifying. Verify in the book.

**Kevin Kelly, "1,000 True Fans" (The Technium, March 4, 2008; later revised)**
- [PRACTITIONER] "A true fan is defined as a fan that will buy anything you produce." If you "keep the full $100 of each true fan, then you need only 1,000 of them to earn $100,000 per year." — [kk.org](https://kk.org/thetechnium/1000-true-fans/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Caveats in the essay: fans "must pay you directly"; 1,000 is an order of magnitude, not a target (the 2008 version suggests "500 True Fans for a painter and 5,000 True Fans for a videomaker"); teams need proportionally more fans; casual fans add income on top. — [kk.org](https://kk.org/thetechnium/1000-true-fans/)

**Seth Godin, *This Is Marketing* (2018)**
- [PRACTITIONER] The publisher blurb says the book teaches you to "identify your smallest viable audience". — [Penguin UK](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/311779/this-is-marketing-by-godin-seth/9780241370155)
- [PRACTITIONER] The excerpt published by StartupNation uses the phrase "**smallest viable market**" and ties it to Kelly's 1,000 true fans. Both phrasings are associated with Godin. — [StartupNation excerpt](https://startupnation.com/books/seth-godin-smallest-viable-market/)
- [PRACTITIONER, unverified quote] Attributed to Godin: "The relentless pursuit of mass will make you boring" (secondary attribution; not checked against the book). — via [search summary of Perell/Shortform pages](https://perell.com/?p=2577)
- [BG] Godin's positioning exercise in the book: draw an XY map with two axes the audience cares about and find an empty "edge". Fill-in-the-blank: "My product is for people who believe ___. I will focus on people who want ___. I promise that engaging with what I make will help you get ___."

**April Dunford, *Obviously Awesome* (2019)**
- [PRACTITIONER] Core components, in order: competitive alternatives ("what customers would do if your solution didn't exist") → unique attributes (only "unique" compared with the alternatives) → value (and proof) → target customer characteristics ("who cares a lot") → market category. The order matters because each step builds on the one before. — [Wudpecker summary](https://www.wudpecker.io/blog/obviously-awesome-the-guide-to-perfecting-your-product-positioning); [Miro template](https://miro.com/miroverse/april-dunfords-positioning-template). Summaries disagree on the count ("five (plus one)" vs "10-step").
- [BG] The book's 10 steps: (1) understand the customers who love you; (2) form a positioning team; (3) align on positioning vs. product as it stands; (4) list true competitive alternatives; (5) isolate unique attributes; (6) map attributes to value themes; (7) decide who cares a lot; (8) find a market frame of reference; (9) optionally layer on a trend; (10) capture the positioning so it can be shared. Verify in the book.

**Paddy Galloway (YouTube strategist; consulted for MrBeast and others)**
- [PRACTITIONER, self-reported agency post] His "category winner" playbook: choose the sub-niche to win first, not the whole niche; start in 1–2 sub-niches and widen as you grow; use repeatable formats; make high-quality long-form content that also works for TV viewers. Case study: Forrest Galante's wildlife channel narrowed to extinct animals, dangerous animals and reptiles, and is reported to have gone "from 30k views/video to 3 million" (+3,900% long-form views). The figures are self-reported. — [YouMind repost of Galloway playbook](https://youmind.com/landing/x-viral-articles/youtube-niche-domination-playbook)
- [PRACTITIONER] Creator Science podcast (Jay Clouse) segments: "Are Some Niches Too Small?" and "Start Narrow and Expand"; Galloway: "I can make any niche viral." — [Creator Science](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/paddy-galloway-2/)
- [BG] "Familiar but unexpected" / "familiar with a twist" as a format principle is widely tied to Colin & Samir's and Galloway's commentary. I could not find a primary source for the exact phrase. Do NOT attribute it verbatim.

**Niche down vs niche up**
- [PRACTITIONER, vendor blog] Subscribr summarizes Galloway's view that early traction is easier in a narrower niche. — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-niche-down-vs-broad)
- [BG] Ali Abdaal has argued that personal-brand creators can be "the niche" themselves (a "niche of one", broad interests filtered through one personality). The counterpoint is that faceless channels have no personality to carry a broad topic set, so they need a tighter topic niche. Not verified in this session.

### Inferences
- For a faceless/mascot channel, the mascot cannot yet be "the niche" because nobody knows it. The topic tilt has to do the positioning work at first, and the character becomes the moat later. Example: "Xa explains the psychology/maths of why gambling hooks you" is a tilt on generic "anti-gambling warnings" (crowded, preachy) and generic "pop psychology" (broad).
- **Interview questions for the skill (niche block):**
  1. What do you know well (job, study, lived experience) and what could you talk about for 100 videos without getting bored? (sweet spot)
  2. Who exactly is the viewer: age, situation, and the moment they would search or stop scrolling? Could you name a real person like this? (smallest viable audience)
  3. What does this viewer watch or do today instead of your channel (other channels, Facebook groups, TikTok, asking friends, nothing)? (Dunford: competitive alternatives)
  4. What can your channel do that those alternatives don't (angle, format, character, depth, local context)? (unique attributes → tilt)
  5. So what? What does the viewer get out of it (understand, feel less alone, avoid losing money, laugh)? (value)
  6. In one sentence: "[Channel] helps [who] [get what] through [format/character], unlike [alternative]." (positioning statement)
  7. Start sub-niches (1–2) vs. later expansion areas. (Galloway)
  8. What would a "true fan" eventually pay for or do (membership, course, book, donate, share)? Is monetization through ads at risk? (Kelly + YouTube ad policy; see Q6)

## 2. What YouTube officially recommends for new channels (strategy, audience, consistency, series/playlists, trailer/about, "algorithm follows audience")

### Takeaway
YouTube's people repeat one line: the recommendation system serves viewers, so target the audience, not "the algorithm". Their concrete tips: look at which videos grow your audience and make more like them; new videos are first understood through metadata and search; avoid over-crowded topics. Official Studio tooling for ideas is now the **Trends tab** (earlier "Research" → "Inspiration"), which surfaces audience searches and "content gaps".

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL, secondhand] Todd Beaupré (YouTube Product Lead, Growth & Discovery) urged creators to replace "algorithm" with "audience" in their questions, because the system is built to serve viewers. — [Search Engine Journal, 2023](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtubes-algorithm-what-you-need-to-know-in-2023/)
- [OFFICIAL, secondhand] Rene Ritchie (YouTube Creator Liaison), Shorts FAQ series (Aug 2024): the algorithm follows the audience, so please the audience. He suggests using the Analytics Audience tab to see which videos are growing your audience and making more like them. — [Tubefilter, 2024-08-26](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/08/26/rene-ritchie-shorts-creator-faqs/)
- [OFFICIAL, secondhand, 2022] Beaupré: with no viewing history, new channels' videos are understood through metadata, and search is likely where fresh videos surface first (title/description), so keyword research matters. For crowded topics he advised a less-trodden path. — [vidIQ blog (vendor), 2022](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-algorithm-secrets/)
- [OFFICIAL, secondhand] VidCon 2022: Beaupré told creators not to obsess over real-time stats and to be patient. MrBeast on the same panel focused on audience over algorithm. — [Tubefilter 2022](https://tubefilter.com/2022/06/24/youtube-mrbeast-vidcon-panel-algorithm-audience-jimmy-donaldson/)
- [OFFICIAL] The Studio **Trends tab** has these cards: **Top searches** (based on your audience and your saves, last 28 days); **Breakout videos** ("well-performing content from similar channels"); **Recent videos** (topics your audience watched, last 28 days); **Content gaps for Shorts**. "A content gap happens when viewers can't find enough quality search results on YouTube for a specific search." Audience interest uses videos watched >1,000 times/week over the last 28 days. "Some of the insights are limited to certain countries, languages, and devices." "Starting July 2026, we are gradually rolling out an updated YouTube Studio experience," so the UI varies. — [YouTube Help 11962757](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11962757)
- [OFFICIAL] An earlier Help page, "Explore Inspiration tab", describes a related-videos card for topics your audience watched in the last 28 days and saved searches, which you can use to "validate an idea's potential". — [YouTube Help 15575509](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15575509)
- [PRACTITIONER, 2021 coverage] The original "Research" tab (Analytics → Research → "Your viewers' searches" / "Searches across YouTube") had content gaps in testing. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-keyword-research/428682)
- [BG] Official YouTube Help/Creators guidance (not fetched this session) also recommends: a channel trailer for non-subscribers; a clear channel description/About; playlists and "series" to organize and encourage binge watching; a consistent schedule communicated to viewers. Creator Insider has said upload frequency per se is not a ranking factor. Verify at [YouTube Help: channel trailer](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3219384) and [YouTube Creators](https://www.youtube.com/creators/).
- [OFFICIAL, from previous note youtube_long.md] Recommendation system optimizes for "valued watchtime" measured by surveys (2021 blog). — [blog.youtube](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/)

### Inferences
- For a brand-new channel, search plus a clear topic-cluster is the first discovery path, which argues for a tight starting niche with searchable "viewer questions" (e.g., "vì sao gỡ mãi không được", "tại sao cờ bạc gây nghiện").
- Trends tab data appears only once a channel has an audience. Before launch, the skill must rely on web/YouTube search, autocomplete and competitor pages.
- Channel-profile.md should record: channel trailer concept, About/description draft (who it's for, what they get, upload rhythm), and planned playlists = content pillars/series.

## 3. Audience definition and research methods

### Takeaway
Define the audience as one specific person in a specific situation with a question ("the viewer's question"), not demographics alone. Collect their real language from comments, forums/groups, autocomplete, Google Trends and, once live, YouTube's Trends tab.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube's own tool frames ideation around "searches" from your audience and "content gaps" where viewers "can't find enough quality search results". — [YouTube Help 11962757](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11962757)
- [OFFICIAL, secondhand] Ritchie: use the Audience tab to find videos that grow your audience. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/08/26/rene-ritchie-shorts-creator-faqs/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Dunford's "who cares a lot" step: target the customer characteristics that make someone care most about your unique value. — [Wudpecker summary](https://www.wudpecker.io/blog/obviously-awesome-the-guide-to-perfecting-your-product-positioning)
- [BG] Jobs-to-be-done (Clayton Christensen, *Competing Against Luck*, 2016): people "hire" a product to make progress in a circumstance. Viewer JTBD examples: "help me understand why I can't stop", "give me something to send to my brother", "make me feel I'm not stupid".
- [BG] Research methods: YouTube search autocomplete (type the seed word + a–z), "People also search", comments on competitor top videos (questions, objections, stories), Reddit/Facebook groups (in VN: groups on cai nghiện, gia đình có người nghiện cờ bạc, tài chính cá nhân), Google Trends (region = Vietnam, compare YouTube Search vs Web Search), TikTok search suggestions, Quora/Voz/Tinhte forums.

### Inferences
- **Audience block of the interview:** primary viewer (age range, life situation, why now); secondary viewer (e.g., family members of gamblers); the viewer's 10 top questions in their own words; what they already believe (myths); what they fear or feel ashamed about; where they hang out (YouTube long, Shorts, TikTok, FB groups); viewing context (commute, late night, with family); tone they trust (doctor, friend, comedian, older brother "anh trai").
- For Xa: the person in the "gambling addiction" niche is often searching privately and feels shame. A non-judgmental mascot can lower that barrier. This is my inference, not a source.

### Gaps
- No Vietnam-specific audience research on gambling-addiction help-seeking found in this session.

## 4. Content pillars, series formats, repeatable format design, posting cadence

### Takeaway
Practitioners converge on 2–4 pillars, each expressed as a named, repeatable format (series) with a fixed structure. YouTube's monetization policy now also requires each episode to be substantively different, so template structure is fine but template content is not (see Q5). Cadence: consistency matters for viewers. I found no official evidence that a specific frequency is rewarded.

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTITIONER] Galloway playbook: "repeatable formats" is one of the 5 steps after choosing a sub-niche. — [YouMind repost](https://youmind.com/landing/x-viral-articles/youtube-niche-domination-playbook)
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube allows the "Same intro and outro for your videos, but the bulk of your content is different". It does NOT allow "Videos where characters are put in the same situation over and over again with the same outcome" or "templated storylines… with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value". — [YouTube Help 1311392 (YPP policies)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- [PRACTITIONER] Pulizzi: build one main channel first ("Building the Base") before diversifying. — [Convince & Convert](https://www.convinceandconvert.com/podcasts/episodes/6-steps-to-monetize-your-content-marketing/)
- [BG] Folklore: "post X times per week". Official YouTube statements (Creator Insider) say frequency is not directly a ranking factor, but consistency helps viewers form habits. Treat any fixed number as [PRACTITIONER] folklore.

### Inferences
- **Pillar/format template for channel-profile.md:** Pillar name → viewer question it answers → series name → fixed beats (hook / question / Xa's explanation / story or case / takeaway / CTA) → length (long 8–15', Shorts <60–180s) → platforms → example 5 titles → what changes every episode (topic, case, data). The last field is the safeguard against the "inauthentic content" rule.
- Example pillars for Xa: (1) "Vì sao não bị dụ?" psychology/neuroscience of near-misses, variable rewards; (2) "Toán của nhà cái" house edge, gambler's fallacy, expected value; (3) "Chuyện thật" anonymized recovery stories and family impact (with consent); (4) "Thoát ra thế nào" help resources, myths about quitting. Shorts = one myth/one mechanism each, linking to the long video.
- Cadence: choose a rhythm the creator can sustain for 6 months (e.g., 1 long/week + 2–3 Shorts) and state it in About. Mark it as a commitment, not an algorithm rule.

### Gaps
- No primary YouTube statement on cadence retrieved; no studies on optimal frequency found.

## 5. Host / mascot persona design and AI/synthetic-content risks

### Takeaway
A mascot needs a written "character bible" so every script, voice and visual stays consistent. YouTube's rules matter here. Animated or clearly unrealistic AI content does NOT need the synthetic-content disclosure label. The July 2025 "inauthentic content" monetization policy, however, targets mass-produced/templated/repetitive AI channels, and the same-character-same-situation pattern is named explicitly.

### Cited Findings
**YouTube "inauthentic content" (YPP monetization policy)**
- [OFFICIAL] July 15, 2025: "We're making a minor update to our 'repetitious content' policy"; "We are also renaming this policy from 'repetitious content' to 'inauthentic content.'"; "There is no change to our reused content policy". — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- [OFFICIAL] Requirement: content must "Not be mass-produced, generic, repetitive, or manipulative." "Generic or repetitive content includes content that looks like it's made with a template". Not allowed: "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production"; "Videos where characters are put in the same situation over and over again with the same outcome"; "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value"; "Channels that heavily rely on generic templates or emotionally manipulative themes". — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- [OFFICIAL] Allowed: "Same intro and outro for your videos, but the bulk of your content is different"; "Content that utilizes creative tools to assist in delivering a unique, well-researched, or creative narrative"; "using AI to edit your video scripts or generate a unique background visual for your content". — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- [OFFICIAL] Reused content: "channels that repurpose content that's already on YouTube or another online source"; also "Content that exclusively features readings of other materials you did not originally create". — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- The page does not explicitly mention AI/TTS narration.

**YouTube altered or synthetic content disclosure**
- [OFFICIAL] Disclosure is required for realistic content that "Makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't do", alters footage of real events/places, or generates realistic scenes that never happened. — [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- [OFFICIAL] No disclosure needed for clearly unrealistic content (examples: a unicorn ride; an AI-generated missile "in a fully animated video") and for minor/production help: "Idea generation", "Production assistance" (outlines, scripts, thumbnails, titles), "Applying beauty filters", and "Cloning one's own voice to create voice overs or dubs". — [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- [OFFICIAL] "Disclosing AI content alone won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money." Creators who consistently fail to disclose may face "manual application of a label, or penalties from YouTube", including "suspension from the YouTube Partner Program." — [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)

**Mascot examples [BG, not verified this session]**
- Duolingo's owl "Duo": the brand's TikTok since ~2021 used an "unhinged", self-aware mascot persona (passive-aggressive reminders, trend participation). Consistent visual + exaggerated, fixed personality.
- Kurzgesagt – In a Nutshell (German studio): recurring flat-design birds as everyman stand-ins. The narrator is a calm, warm voice, and the birds are visual identity rather than a talking host. Heavy sourcing (public source docs per video).
- VTubers (e.g., Hololive, Nijisanji): an avatar plus a human voice performer with a fixed "lore" (backstory, age, catchphrases, fan names). This shows that audiences bond with a consistent character driven by a real performer.
- CGP Grey: faceless, stick-figure avatar; animation + voice-over explainer, 5–10 min. — mentioned in [Cellphones S-Forum list](https://cellphones.com.vn/sforum/top-kenh-youtube-hay-nhat) (secondary)

### Inferences
**Character bible checklist (for channel-profile.md "Host/Mascot" section):**
1. Name, species/form, one-line concept ("Xa, a snake who used to be a nhà cái's mascot and now explains the tricks").
2. Role vs. viewer: teacher / older sibling / reformed insider / curious friend. Who is the "straight man" if there's a second character?
3. 3–5 personality traits + one flaw (e.g., sarcastic but kind, nerdy about numbers, hates being called "rắn độc").
4. Values / stance: what Xa believes (no shaming, people are not stupid, the system is designed to win).
5. Voice: pronouns (Xa xưng "tớ/mình/anh" – gọi người xem "cậu/bạn/các bạn"), register, regional accent (Bắc/Nam), speech rate, TTS voice ID or human VA, signature sound ("xì…").
6. Catchphrases / recurring segments (opening line, sign-off, a recurring "đố biết" question).
7. Never-do list: never promote/name/link betting sites, never give "winning tips", never mock victims, never give medical diagnosis, never break character in a way that hurts trust, never use real people's likeness without consent.
8. Visual identity: colour palette, silhouette, 3–5 fixed expressions/poses, props, thumbnail style; reference sheet for consistency across AI/animation tools.
9. Backstory/lore (light), plus how it can grow across series.
10. Platform adaptation: long-form Xa (explainer) vs. Shorts/TikTok Xa (faster, meme-able) vs. Facebook (community, text posts in Xa's voice).
11. Disclosure & authenticity plan: AI tools used (script help, visuals, TTS); whether any realistic AI imagery of real people/events appears (→ disclose); how each episode differs substantively (topic, data, case) to stay clear of the "same situation… same outcome" clause.
12. Human-in-the-loop: who researches/fact-checks; sources list per video (Kurzgesagt-style).

- Risk for Xa: a recurring snake character with a fixed "lesson" each episode could resemble "characters… in the same situation over and over again with the same outcome" if the scripts are formulaic. Mitigation: vary the topic, evidence and narrative per episode, and add genuine commentary/education.

## 6. Competitor / gap analysis methods

### Takeaway
Find 5–15 channels the target viewer already watches. For each, record a few public numbers (subs, upload frequency, top videos, outlier videos where views far exceed subscriber count), title/thumbnail patterns, and what viewers ask for in comments that nobody answers. Web search can show what exists. It cannot show retention, CTR or revenue.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] The Trends tab "Breakout videos" card shows "well-performing content from similar channels". This is the official version of outlier hunting, available only after you have an audience. — [YouTube Help 11962757](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11962757)
- [OFFICIAL] "Content gaps" are searches with insufficient quality results; some insights are limited by country/language/device. — [YouTube Help 11962757](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11962757)
- [PRACTITIONER, vendor] Social Blade publishes public subscriber/view rankings by country (e.g., top Vietnamese channels). — [Social Blade VN top list](https://socialblade.com/youtube/lists/top/100/subscribers/all/VN). vidIQ publishes channel stat pages (vendor), e.g. [vidIQ channel stats](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCUUkpAvYpmYUPajHaL3Da0w).
- [BG] Free methods: YouTube search with filters (Type: Channel; Upload date; Sort by view count), the channel "Popular" sort, the "Videos" tab to estimate cadence, autocomplete, and related/"up next" chains from a seed video. vidIQ/TubeBuddy (vendors, freemium browser extensions) show an "outlier"/views-per-hour or tags. Treat their scores as vendor estimates.

### Inferences
- **Competitor record template** (per channel): name + URL; language/country; format (faceless/mascot/host); subs; total videos; uploads in last 90 days; avg views of last 10 videos; top 3 videos (views, age); outlier ratio = views ÷ subs (flag >1 as outliers); title patterns (question/number/"Vì sao…"/story); thumbnail pattern; recurring series; tone; what comments repeatedly ask for; what's missing (gap); what to borrow (format) vs. avoid.
- **Limits to state in the profile:** web search and Claude's fetch cannot see retention, CTR, traffic sources, revenue or demographics. Subscriber counts are rounded. View counts on fetched pages may be stale. TikTok/Facebook stats are often unavailable without login. Treat findings as directional.
- Skill behaviour: optional web step to search "[topic] YouTube" in Vietnamese + English, list candidate channels, ask the user to confirm/add the channels they know, then fill the template. The skill should not fabricate numbers it could not see and should write "unknown" instead.

### Gaps
- No primary methodology study on outlier ratio thresholds; vidIQ/TubeBuddy feature specifics not verified this session.

## 7. Sensitive niches (gambling, mental health, finance, health): YouTube policy + Vietnam legal context

### Takeaway
An anti-gambling/addiction-education channel is allowed and can earn ads with educational context. It must never point viewers to gambling sites, never promise returns, and should avoid graphic self-harm depictions. In Vietnam, advertising illegal gambling websites is banned under the Advertising Law, with fines of 70–100 million VND for individuals. Knowingly taking money to promote such sites can be prosecuted as complicity in "Tổ chức đánh bạc" (Art. 322 Penal Code).

### Cited Findings
**YouTube gambling rules (Community Guidelines, March 2025 update)**
- [OFFICIAL, via press] From March 19, 2025, YouTube does not allow content that directs viewers to "unapproved" gambling sites (those not meeting local legal requirements and not reviewed by Google/YouTube) via links, pinned comments, logos or verbal pointers. Content promising guaranteed returns may be removed even when the site is approved. Content depicting/promoting online casinos becomes age-restricted (no under-18s or logged-out viewers). — [SBC Americas, 2025-03-04](https://sbcamericas.com/2025/03/04/youtube-ban-content-unapproved-gambling/); [ABC7 News](https://abc7news.com/amp/post/youtube-cracks-down-gambling-videos-trying-lure-viewers-unapproved-sites/15976808/); [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250305-youtube-online-gambling-rules)
- [BG] The policy text lives in YouTube's "Sale of illegal or regulated goods or services" policy. Verify at [YouTube Help 9229611](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9229611). My fetch of answer/12441148 returned 404.

**Advertiser-friendly guidelines (monetization)**

**Vietnam legal context**
- [OFFICIAL, via VnExpress 2025-09-26] Khoản 1 Điều 7 Luật Quảng cáo 2012: "hàng hóa, dịch vụ cấm kinh doanh theo quy định của pháp luật sẽ bị cấm quảng cáo", so advertising illegal gambling websites is banned. Administrative fine (khoản 2 Điều 5, điểm a khoản 2 và khoản 3 Điều 33 Nghị định 38/2021/NĐ-CP, sửa đổi bởi Nghị định 128/2022/NĐ-CP): cá nhân "phạt tiền từ 70 đến 100 triệu đồng" + buộc gỡ nội dung; tổ chức 140–200 triệu. Unknowing violators are still fined, with mitigation considered. Anyone who knowingly takes sponsorship/commission can be prosecuted as an accomplice under Điều 322 BLHS ("Tổ chức đánh bạc hoặc gá bạc"): lowest bracket fine 50–300 triệu or 1–5 years' imprisonment. — [VnExpress](https://vnexpress.net/quang-cao-cho-website-danh-bac-trai-phep-bi-xu-phat-the-nao-4943567-p2.html)

- [BG] Legal gambling in VN is narrow: Vietlott lottery, licensed casinos (mostly for foreigners; pilot for Vietnamese at Phú Quốc/Vân Đồn under Nghị định 03/2017), and pilot sports betting (Nghị định 06/2017). ### Inferences
- **Sensitive-niche checklist for the profile:** (1) no links/logos/names/codes of betting sites, even "as examples" (blur them in screenshots); (2) no "kèo", no "winning strategy", no return promises; (3) educational framing stated in the intro and description; (4) no graphic self-harm imagery; dramatize non-graphically; (5) add help resources (hotlines/organizations, verified) in description; (6) cite sources; no medical diagnosis or treatment claims beyond health-authority consensus; (7) reject sponsorships from any gambling/"game bài đổi thưởng" brand (legal exposure under Art. 322 + ad law); (8) label any paid partnership per the 2025 ad-law amendment; (9) expect "limited ads" on some episodes. Plan memberships/sponsors from health/finance-literacy brands instead.
- The same checklist pattern (policy + local law + never-do list) generalizes to finance (scam/guaranteed returns, securities advice), health (medical misinformation) and mental health (self-harm, suicide guidance).
